## (a) Which AI-native lending and brokerage platforms exist, what their agents automate, what controls they use, and what results they claim

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the CRE debt "broker OS" category is real. Lev is the clearest AI-first rival to an AI-run broker: it covers intake, extraction, underwriting models, lender research and outreach, closing, and (since Oct 2026) deal sourcing. CommLoan and Janover Pro market AI lender matching. Finance Lobby is a passive lender directory. On the lender side, the agents that work best are narrow and tied to an SOP: Casca for SBA intake and eligibility, Built's Draw Agent for construction draws, Clik.ai for CRE spreading, Ocrolus for document AI, and Aloan for credit memos. Human-in-the-loop usually comes as "assist, then automate" modes and citations back to source documents, not full autonomy. Almost all claimed results come from the vendors themselves.

### Inferences
- Task coverage across the market: intake/pre-qual (Casca, Parlay, Lev), document collection and chasing (Casca), extraction and spreading (Clik.ai, Ocrolus, Aloan, Lev), credit-memo drafting with citations (Aloan, Lev Agent "drafts materials"), lender matching (CommLoan CUPID, Janover Pro, Lev, YieldStack), outreach (Lev, Lev Source), draws/servicing (Built). **Condition clearing and closing-checklist agents for brokers were not evidenced for any vendor.** That is a plausible white space for Kubera.
- No vendor found sends outbound lender submissions with zero human review. The common pattern is graduated autonomy per task (Built's audit/assist/automate) plus source citations (Aloan). Kubera's "every outbound agent action passes a broker approval gate" matches this pattern and is defensible. Adding a per-task autonomy level (audit, then assist, then automate) would let low-risk actions such as document-request reminders graduate out of the gate later.
- Lev is the closest direct competitor on the broker workflow. Kubera's differentiation would likely need to come from niche program depth (HUD 232/223(f)/221(d)(4), SBA 504/7(a) with Form 159, hotel PIP/CMBS) and registered lender credit boxes with all-in-cost quotes. Generic CRE debt search is already commoditized (Finance Lobby is free, Lev starts at roughly $80/mo).
- Lender-count claims (900 vs 300 for CommLoan; 4,000, 7,000, 10,000 elsewhere) are not comparable or verified. A smaller, structured, verified credit box beats a large, unstructured directory for matching quality.

## (b) Third-party data and service integrations for a commercial brokerage (with indicative pricing)

### Takeaway
Most of the data a broker's agents need can be bought through APIs, but pricing is mostly behind sales calls and annual contracts. The cheap or free building blocks are FEMA flood data, the OFAC SDN list, OpenCorporates (paid for commercial use), and Regrid self-serve. The expensive or contract-gated ones are ATTOM, Ocrolus, Plaid Assets, Middesk, bureau credit, and LightBox Collateral360. A broker at low deal volume should buy per transaction where it can (e.g., single bureau reports, Proof per-notarization), and leave appraisal/third-party report ordering to the lender's platform (often Collateral360) while tracking it.

### Inferences
- **The broker vs. lender split matters.** In CRE and SBA, the lender usually orders the appraisal, environmental report, PCA and flood cert, often through Collateral360, and the title/escrow company issues prelims. For Kubera, the agent's job is mostly *tracking* vendor orders (status, ETA, cost, who pays) and *pre-screening* (property/ownership lookup, FEMA zone, entity standing, OFAC on all principals) before a lender submission. That favors cheap per-call sources over enterprise contracts.
- A low-cost stack for a broker at current volume: Regrid self-serve or ATTOM entry tier for parcel/ownership; FEMA NFHL (free) for a flood *pre-screen* only, not a Standard Flood Hazard Determination; OpenCorporates or a SOS aggregator plus manual CA bizfile lookups for entity status; public OFAC SDN download or a low-cost per-query API (log every screen with list version/date); per-report bureau credit via a reseller with signed guarantor authorization; Plaid only if borrower-permissioned bank data adds value over uploaded statements; Proof per-transaction for RON.
- Ocrolus at about $48k+/yr is hard to justify against Kubera's planned in-house extraction (NeMo Retriever plus Claude). Clik.ai is a possible integrate option for T12/rent-roll spreading if in-house accuracy lags.

## (c) Backend patterns for safe agentic workflows in regulated lending, and regulator/auditor expectations

### Takeaway
The established 2026 pattern is a durable workflow engine (Inngest or Temporal) wrapped around every agent loop. It journals each LLM and tool call, keeps idempotent tool boundaries, and pauses on human-approval events with timeouts and three outcomes (approved, rejected, no response), backed by versioned prompts and tools. For regulation, the big 2024–25 AI-specific state rules have been pushed back or narrowed. Colorado's SB 24-205 was replaced in May 2026 by a narrower disclosure law effective 2027. California's ADMT rules bite on Jan 1, 2027 for decisions about "financial or lending services." CFPB 1071 compliance moved to Jan 1, 2028. The ECOA/Reg B duty to give specific adverse-action reasons still applies whatever the model. A broker is not usually the creditor, but it will be asked by lenders and auditors for decision trails.

### Cited Findings
**Durable workflows and approval gates**
- Inngest documents human-in-the-loop for agents. `step.waitForEvent()` pauses execution until a correlated approval event arrives (from Slack, email or a dashboard), matched on an `approvalId` expression with `timeout: "24h"`. The docs say to handle approved, rejected and no-response outcomes. Waiting runs hold no worker or connection — [Inngest docs: HITL (snippet)](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/durable-agents/human-in-the-loop); [Inngest waitForEvent reference (snippet)](https://www.inngest.com/docs/reference/typescript/v4/functions/step-wait-for-event.md)
- Inngest documents gating only *risky* tools inside the agent loop by checking tool names against an approval list. Agent tool sandboxes are in beta, and the docs recommend pausing the sandbox during approval waits. Inngest TypeScript SDK v4 is current — [Inngest durable agents docs (snippet)](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/durable-agents)
- Temporal "Agent Harness" (Aug 2026 post) runs each agent as a Temporal Workflow with tool-call approvals. It integrates with Gemini, OpenAI Agents SDK and PydanticAI — [Temporal blog (snippet)](https://temporal.io/blog/temporal-agent-harness-durable-agent-infrastructure). A Vercel AI SDK integration (Jan 2026) wraps LLM calls as retryable Activities — [Temporal blog (snippet)](https://temporal.io/blog/building-durable-agents-with-temporal-and-ai-sdk-by-vercel). Event History durably records the events that drive execution, which makes it an event-sourced audit substrate — same source.
- 2026 guides compare Inngest, Temporal, Restate and DBOS. They list the requirements for durable agents as an execution journal, **idempotent tool boundaries, versioned prompts and tools, durable human approvals, and recovery tests** — [zylos.ai research note 2026-04-24 (snippet)](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-24-durable-execution-agent-runtimes); [frenchydigital 2026 guide (snippet)](https://frenchydigital.com/blog/ai-agent-durable-execution-2026)
- Unverified secondary claim: Temporal raised a $300M Series D at a $5B valuation (Feb 2026) — single secondary source via [search summary](https://frenchydigital.com/blog/ai-agent-durable-execution-2026).

**Regulation**

About 13 data fields (down from 81). ### Inferences
- **Map Kubera's plan onto the pattern.** Inngest functions per deal workflow. Each agent action becomes a `step.run` with an idempotency key (dealId + action + version). Outbound actions (email, SMS, lender submission, CRM write that changes stage/fees) go through `step.waitForEvent("broker.approval", { if: approvalId, timeout })` with explicit "expired → re-queue, never auto-send" handling. Read-only tools (property lookup, OFAC screen, extraction) run ungated but logged.
- **Make the audit trail its own append-only store, not only Inngest run history.** Inngest/Temporal history retention is limited and vendor-hosted. Kubera needs an immutable `agent_events` table (Supabase/Postgres, insert-only via RLS and no UPDATE/DELETE grants, optional hash chain) recording: actor (agent/model/version or human), prompt/template version, inputs (by reference to vault doc IDs), tool called, output, approver, approval timestamp, and the external message ID returned by Resend/Telnyx/Zoho.
- **Lender matching and the qualifier are the "decision" surfaces.** As a broker, Kubera is generally not the ECOA creditor that issues adverse action. [background, not verified this session] Reg B's "creditor" definition (12 CFR 1002.2(l)) does reach persons who regularly refer applicants to creditors for the anti-discrimination and anti-discouragement provisions (§1002.4(a)–(b)). So an AI qualifier that tells a sponsor "you don't qualify" or steers them away should be explainable from rule-based "Jev" reasons and must never use prohibited bases. Kubera should keep a reason-code log for every qualifier and match output.
- **CCPA/ADMT applicability is uncertain and fact-dependent.** It applies only if Kubera meets the CCPA "business" thresholds [background: revenue threshold around $26.6M (2025-adjusted), or 100k+ consumers, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing PI, not verified this session]. It would also apply to individuals (guarantors, sponsors) who are California residents. If covered, AI that decides whether a person gets lending services would trigger pre-use notice, opt-out and access by Jan 1, 2027. The cheap insurance is to keep a human broker as the decision-maker of record (the approval gate), which also supports a "human review" posture under ADMT. Counsel should confirm.
- **Retention:** [background, not verified this session] CA DRE requires brokers to keep transaction records for 3 years (Bus. & Prof. Code §10148). Reg B §1002.12(b) sets 12 months for business-credit application records (25 months for consumer). SBA Form 159 records should follow SBA SOP. Design the vault and audit log for at least 3 years by default, plus legal hold.
- **PII/data residency:** guarantor SSNs, DOBs, credit reports and bank statements should be field-encrypted or tokenized. They should not be sent to market-intel models (xAI Grok) or any model without a zero-retention/no-training agreement. The model gateway should enforce routing by data classification, not only by cost.
- **Evaluation and monitoring:** Langfuse tracing should carry deal ID and approval ID so a reviewer can go from a sent email to the trace that produced it. Maintain golden-set evals for extraction (T12, rent roll, PFS, tax returns) and lender-match regression tests before every prompt or model change.

### Gaps
- Langfuse vs Braintrust capabilities and pricing, MCP security guidance (tool scoping, prompt injection from inbound email/documents), and Twenty CRM MCP specifics were not searched within the budget.
- Colorado SB26-189 coverage of commercial and consumer lending was not confirmed. The CFPB circular status (withdrawn vs "2026-03") is unresolved.
- California-specific commercial financing disclosure (SB 1235/DFPI) applicability to a DRE broker arranging real-estate-secured commercial loans was not researched.
