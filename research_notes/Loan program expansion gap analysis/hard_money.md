## 1. Products and underwriting: the hard money menu, typical terms, and how underwriting differs from bank or agency lending

### Takeaway
Private and hard money loans are short-term, interest-only, balloon loans sized mainly on collateral value: as-is LTV, after-repair value (ARV) or loan-to-cost. Rates run about 9.5–13%, points about 1–4, and terms 6–36 months, with extension fees, default interest and minimum-interest prepayment terms. Underwriting turns on the asset, the exit strategy, the scope of work and borrower experience, not on global DSCR or credit as in bank or agency lending. Sourcing for the commercial-specific numbers (construction LTC, land, second liens, rescue capital) is thin; most published figures are fix-and-flip or residential-investor oriented.

### Cited Findings
**Rates, points and terms**
- One lender-data summary reports an average hard money rate of 9.95% in Q1 2026 — [Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/hard-money-loan-rates-2026) (search snippet)
- Broker guides put the typical range at 8–15% a year, depending on property type, borrower experience, LTV and lender. Another guide puts hard money bridge loans at 9.5–12.5% — [JetLending 2026 guide](https://www.jetlending.com/blog/hard-money-bridge-loans-a-2026-guide-for-real-estate-investors); [Slate Financial](https://www.slatefinancial.io/blog/wp-bridge-loan-vs-hard-money-loan-2026) (snippets; lender marketing)
- Points: most sources cite 1–4 points. One dataset reports an average of 2.96 points — [Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/hard-money-loan-rates-2026); [Private Lender Link](https://privatelenderlink.com/hard-money-loans/) (snippets)
- Leverage, fix-and-flip: commonly 65–75% of ARV. One guide gives 65–75% as-is and 70–80% of ARV. Another says the ceiling is typically 70%, with a few lenders reaching 75% LTV — [Wonder Rates](https://wonderrates.com/learning-hub/hard-money-loans-vs-bridge-loans); [Slate Financial fix-and-flip](https://www.slatefinancial.io/blog/wp-bridge-loan-vs-hard-money-fix-and-flip-2026-32) (snippets)
- Leverage, bridge: up to 80–85% for stabilized assets. One source says some private bridge lenders go to 85–90% LTV at 13–16%+ with full recourse — [YieldStack multifamily bridge LTV 2026](https://yieldstack.ai/blog/multifamily-bridge-loan-ltv); [Slate Financial](https://www.slatefinancial.io/blog/wp-bridge-loan-vs-hard-money-loan-2026) (snippets)
- Term: hard money typically runs 6–24 months, interest-only with a balloon at maturity. Bridge loans typically run 12–36 months — [Slate Financial](https://www.slatefinancial.io/blog/wp-bridge-loan-vs-hard-money-loan-2026) (snippet)

**Extensions, default and prepayment**
- Extensions: many lenders offer 3–6-month extensions for 0.5–1% of the balance. Another source gives 0.5–2.0% per extension period. Granting an extension is at the lender's discretion — [Crowdfunded Wealth (SEC filings analysis, 2026)](https://www.crowdfundedwealth.com/articles/hard-money-loan-extension-default-interest-points-2026); [OfferMarket term sheet guide](https://www.offermarket.us/blog/hard-money-loan-term-sheet) (snippets)
- Default interest: an analysis of public lenders' SEC filings found the default rate is often the greater of the stated rate and prime + 3.0%. A default rate of up to 24% a year appears in the same dataset. This is a ceiling seen in some agreements, not a market average — [Crowdfunded Wealth](https://www.crowdfundedwealth.com/articles/hard-money-loan-extension-default-interest-points-2026) (snippet)
- Prepayment: some lenders require 3–6 months of minimum interest. Exit fees run 0.5–1% at payoff. Origination runs 1–3 points — [Crowdfunded Wealth](https://www.crowdfundedwealth.com/articles/hard-money-loan-extension-default-interest-points-2026); [OfferMarket](https://www.offermarket.us/blog/hard-money-loan-term-sheet) (snippets)

**California LTV caps on broker-arranged investor loans (relevant to land and junior liens)**
- When a broker places investor money, California caps LTV under B&P §10232.3(a) at 80% of current market value for improved real property and 50% for unimproved real property. A single-family-zoned lot is capped at 65%. Each parcel securing a loan must meet its own limit — [DRE Mortgage Loan Broker Compliance Evaluation Manual (RE 7)](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true) (snippet; older DRE document). The same figures appear in bill text referencing §§10232.3, 10232.45 and 10238 — [SB 647 bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=201520160SB647) (snippet). I did not confirm whether these limits apply to all broker-arranged investor loans or only to particular exemptions; see the California rules section.

**How underwriting differs**
- Approval centers on property value and the exit plan more than on credit. ARV is described as the key metric. Example: a 75% loan against a $300,000 ARV leaves a $75,000 equity cushion — [OfferMarket](https://www.offermarket.us/blog/what-is-a-hard-cash-loan); [Capital Funding approval criteria](https://capitalfunding.com/blog/hard-money-lender-approval-criteria-top-10-factors/) (snippets)
- Exit strategy: lenders want a specific, time-bound repayment plan. A refinance exit backed by a pre-qualification from a takeout lender is stronger than a sale exit with no buyer interest documented. Most borrowers either sell or refinance into term debt such as DSCR loans — [Biz2Credit requirements guide](https://biz2credit.com/hard-money-loans/requirements-guide); [FlipperForce](https://flipperforce.com/how-to-flip-houses/chapter-3-getting-funding-for-your-flip-projects/fix-and-flip-hard-money-loans-explained) (snippets)
- Scope of work is a hard requirement and must reconcile to the ARV: if the valuation assumes an updated kitchen, the budget and timeline must include it — [BiggerPockets thread](https://www.biggerpockets.com/posts/7315489) (snippet; forum)
- Borrower experience drives tiering. One snippet defines Tier 1 as 0–1 completed projects; I could not find the other tier definitions — [Biz2Credit qualifications](https://www.biz2credit.com/hard-money-loans/qualifications-real-estate-investors) (snippet)
- Feasibility stress tests: does the deal survive rehab overruns and a longer carry? Hold a 10–20% contingency on rehab budgets — [New Funding Resources](https://newfundingresources.com/?p=11891); [Anchor Loans](https://anchorloans.com/blog/understanding-the-role-of-hard-money-lenders-in-complex-real-estate-projects) (snippets)

### Inferences
- Product menu for Kubera's sizer. This is a synthesis; the specific numbers are illustrative and not sourced except where cited above.
  - **Commercial bridge:** as-is LTV about 65–75%; 12–24 months plus extensions.
  - **Fix-and-flip:** LTARV about 65–75%, LTC about 85–90% of purchase plus 100% of rehab held back in draws.
  - **Fix-and-hold:** bridge to a DSCR takeout.
  - **Ground-up construction:** LTC, LTARV and an interest reserve. These are the standard sizing constraints, but I found no 2026 sourced LTC benchmark.
  - **Land:** low LTV; the 50% California cap applies when broker-placed investor money funds unimproved land.
  - **Cash-out on non-stabilized assets:** priced off as-is value.
  - **Rescue / distressed:** maturity defaults, discounted payoffs, note purchases.
  - **Second liens:** combined LTV, subordinate to the senior lender's consent.
- Kubera's current marketing claims private bridge loans of $750k–$25M closing in 10–21 days. That is consistent with commercial bridge norms, but the sizer needs hard money parameters that do not exist in an agency or SBA sizer: ARV, LTC, interest reserve, holdback, extension options, default rate and minimum interest.
- The underwriting inputs that differ from Kubera's current programs (HUD, SBA, agency, CMBS) are ARV appraisal or broker price opinion, budget and scope of work, borrower track-record schedule, exit analysis (takeout DSCR or sale comps), and draw schedule. Global DSCR, tax returns and personal financial statements matter less.

### Gaps
- No sourced 2025–2026 benchmarks for commercial (not 1–4 unit) ground-up construction LTC, land LTV in the private market, interest reserve sizing, or second-lien CLTV. AAPL and Scotsman Guide data pages were not reachable.
- I found no source for late-charge conventions. California late-charge limits on business-purpose loans were not researched.
- The rate and point figures come mainly from lender marketing blogs; treat them as indicative.

## 2. Capital side: table funding, a broker-controlled fund, fractional / multi-lender trust deeds, investor onboarding and portals

### Takeaway
A California broker has three main capital models:
- **Table funding:** a capital partner funds the loan in its own name or the broker's at close.
- **A pooled fund:** an LLC or LP that issues securities to investors and lends from its balance sheet.
- **Whole or fractional trust deeds:** sold directly to individual investors, either under the DRE multi-lender rules (§10237–10238, at most 10 investors) or under a Corporations Code exemption.

Each model brings a different licensing, securities and servicing burden. Investor onboarding requires suitability documentation (RE 870 questionnaire, net-worth tests) and lender/purchaser disclosure (RE 851A/851B) before funds are accepted.

### Cited Findings
- **Table funding:** another party's capital funds the loan at closing. In California, a DRE-licensed originator must disclose the actual funder and the party that will hold the deed of trust. An originator with only a California Finance Lender (CFL) license must fund from its own balance sheet and may not use trust deed investors or other capital providers — [Private Lender Link table-funding guide](https://privatelenderlink.com/guide/table-funding/) (snippet)
- **Fractional trust deeds:** several investors share one note and deed of trust. One example is a second-position loan with about 10 investors. CrowdTrustDeed is a platform example: investors can buy whole or fractional portions, and a third party services the loans — [BiggerPockets fractional thread](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/70/topics/352493-fractional-private-money-loan); [CrowdTrustDeed legal page](https://crowdtrustdeed.com/legal/) (snippets)
- **Fund model:** one lender's marketing cites diversification, a $50K+ minimum, professional loan administration and reduced single-property risk. This is a sales claim from one firm, not an independent comparison — [ez-loans investors page](https://ez-loans.pages.dev/investors) (snippet; low reliability)
- **Multi-lender cap:** DRE material says multi-lender ("fractionalized") transactions arranged by a broker are exempt from Corporate Securities Law qualification only if they have no more than ten lenders or note purchasers, each with a qualifying net worth or income — [DRE Reference Book, Agency chapter](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/refbook/ref10.pdf) (snippet)
- **Onboarding documents:**
  - RE 851A (loan origination: the investor funds a new loan) and RE 851B (the investor buys an existing note). RE 851D is the multi-property addendum — [DRE MLB forms](https://dre.ca.gov/Forms/MLB.html); [Doss Law disclosure and DRE filing summary](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/private-trust-deed-lending-disclosure-and-dre-filing-summary/) (snippets)
  - The investor must sign before the broker accepts investor funds — [Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/private-trust-deed-lending-disclosure-and-dre-filing-summary/); [Dan Harkey](https://danharkey.com/posts/deed-of-trust-investments-in-california-legal-framework-and-licensing-requirements) (snippets)
  - Suitability falls under §10232.45. The investor questionnaire is RE 870. Investor qualification statements under §10238(f) must be kept for 4 years — [DRE RE 7 manual](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true) (snippet)
  - The investor states that the investment does not exceed 10% of net worth, excluding home, furnishings and automobiles. Doss Law says this requirement under §10232.3 applies whatever exemption is used — [Doss Law alternatives to multi-lender rule](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/alternatives-to-the-multi-lender-rule/) (snippet; about 4 years old)
  - The broker must record in the transaction file which code section or securities exemption the deal relies on (§10236.7) and tell investors — [Justia BPC §10236.7](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-5/section-10236-7/) (snippet). The DRE requires the exemption choice to be declared in the written disclosures and loan file, and provides no model form for it — [Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/private-trust-deed-lending-disclosure-and-dre-filing-summary/) (snippet)
- **Investor portals and distributions:**
  - The Mortgage Office (TMO) advertises self-service investor portals, automated distributions, investor splits and participation tracking, and preferred-return calculations — [TMO private lending page](https://www.themortgageoffice.com/industries/private-lending/); [TMO Fund Management](https://themortgageoffice.com/products/fund-management-software) (snippets)
  - Mortgage Automator advertises automated distribution and reinvestment calculations and investor statements — [GetApp Mortgage Automator](https://www.getapp.com/finance-accounting-software/a/mortgage-automator/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For Kubera, which advertises white-label table funding and lists a private fund among its routing targets, the lowest-friction path is: (1) keep table funding with institutional or private-credit partners, and disclose the actual funder per the table-funding guidance; then (2) add whole-loan and fractional placements to individual investors under §10237–10238 once the RE 851A, suitability and servicing workflows exist; then (3) treat a Kubera-sponsored pooled fund as a separate securities project requiring securities counsel.
- Investor onboarding is a regulated workflow, not just a CRM contact type. It needs accreditation and suitability capture, net-worth tests, RE 870 and RE 851A/B generation and e-signature, per-loan allocation (for example 10 investors at most per note under the multi-lender exemption), funds-in tracking to a trust account, and an exemption declaration in the loan file.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how investors are allocated in practice (first-come vs pro rata vs reservation) or on typical minimums for California fractional trust deeds.
- The fund-model economics (management fee, spread, preferred return) and how 3(c)(5)(C) and the Advisers Act apply to a broker-sponsored California mortgage fund were not confirmed; securities counsel is required.

## 3. California regulatory rules when a DRE broker arranges or funds private-money loans

### Takeaway
A DRE broker can lawfully arrange, and in some cases fund, business-purpose private loans. Arranging solicited loans and selling notes to investors (§10131(d) and (e)) layers on the following:
- **Article 5** (§10230 et seq.): LTV caps, investor suitability, threshold-broker notice and annual reporting, servicing.
- **Article 6** (§10237–10238): multi-lender rules, at most 10 investors per note.
- **Article 7** (§10240): the Mortgage Loan Disclosure Statement (MLDS), which the DRE says applies to any type of property, commercial included.
- **10 CCR §2830 et seq.:** trust-account rules.

Civil Code §1916.1 exempts broker-made or broker-arranged real-property loans from usury. SB 1146 (effective 2025-01-01) extends the exemption to modifications and extensions that a broker arranges. Selling fractional interests outside the DRE multi-lender rules, or running a pooled fund, triggers California and federal securities law. A CFL license is an alternative or complementary lender license. Several thresholds below conflict across sources and must be checked against the current statutes.

### Cited Findings
**Licensing activity (§10131(d) and (e)) and the CFL interplay**
- The California Mortgage Association notes that B&P §10131(d) includes commercial financing transactions in the broker definition — [CMA comment letter to DFPI, PRO 01-18](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/04/PRO-01-18-California-Mortgage-Association.pdf) (snippet). I did not obtain the statute text of §10131(d) (soliciting and negotiating loans, and servicing them, for compensation) or §10131(e) (buying, selling or exchanging notes for others).
- The CFL does not apply to any loan made or arranged by a licensed real estate broker and secured by real property, or to a licensed broker making such a loan (Fin. Code §22050). The exemption also lets a broker sell such a loan to a finance lender or arrange for a finance lender to make it — [Paul Hastings CFL alert](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/the-california-financing-law-commercial-and-consumer-lenders-beware); [§22050 summary](https://tofighlaw.legalfina.com/en/laws/ca/financial/california_financing_law/general_provisions/exemptions) (snippets)
- §22050(e) exempts a person who makes five or fewer commercial loans in a 12-month period incidental to their business. The single-loan exemption expired January 1, 2022, which affected single-project SPVs — [Holland & Knight 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/05/california-revives-de-minimis-lender-license-exemption); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/financial-services-law/exemption-from-california-lender-licensing-law-for) (snippets)
- A CFL finance-broker-only license allows brokering only to CFL lenders. Brokering mortgage loans to non-CFL lenders generally needs a DRE broker license or a CRMLA license — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/the-california-financing-law-commercial-and-consumer-lenders-beware) (snippet). A commercial loan under the CFL is $5,000 or more and used primarily for business purposes — [First Tuesday MLO mentor](https://journal.firsttuesday.us/mlo-mentor-california-financing-law/83433/) (snippet)
- CFL licensees are "exempt persons" from California's constitutional usury limits under Fin. Code §22002 — [calcorporatelaw.com "Usury exemption bites CFL licensee"](https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/2010/10/usury-exemption-bites-cfl-licensee) (snippet; 2010). **Pending:** AB 2116 (2025–26 session) would amend §22002 with an operative date of 2028-01-01. Its content and status are unverified — [LegiScan AB2116](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2116/2025) (snippet)

**Article 5: threshold broker (§10232) and annual reports (§10232.2)**
- A broker who meets the §10232 criteria must notify the DRE in writing within 30 days (form RE 853) — [DRE Reference Book ch. 12](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/refbook/ref12.pdf); [DRE MLB forms](https://www.dre.ca.gov/forms/mlb.html) (snippets)
- **Threshold criteria. The sources conflict.**
  - One copy of current §10232 reads "a combination of 10 or more" transactions under §10131(d)/(e) or §10131.1 "in an aggregate amount of more than one million dollars ($1,000,000)", plus a prong for collections of $250,000 or more on behalf of note owners — [FindLaw BPC §10232](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-10232/) / [Onecle](https://law.onecle.com/california/business/10232.html) (snippets)
  - The RE 853 form language refers to notes aggregating more than $1,000,000 and collections of $250,000 or more in the past 12 months — [RE 853](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/forms/re853.pdf) (snippet)
  - A 1991–92 bill used 20 or more transactions over $2,000,000 — [AB 3121 (1991–92)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=199119920AB3121) (snippet)
  - A tax-prep site gives yet another version — [TaxShark](https://taxsharkinc.com/how-to-fill-out-california-form-re-851-w-examples-faqs/) (low reliability)
  - **Counsel must confirm the current numbers and look-back periods.**
- Annual report: a threshold broker files the Business Activity Report within 90 days after its fiscal year ends — [DRE RE 881 preview](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/forms/re881_preview.pdf); [RE 881 instructions](https://bars.dre.ca.gov/pdfs/re881_Instructions.pdf) (snippets)
- Trust fund status reporting: one snippet says the threshold broker's annual report satisfies the trust account review requirement — [DRE RE 7](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf) (snippet)
- Quarterly reporting and CPA review: the DRE consumer pamphlet says multi-lender servicing agreements must require a CPA to inspect the servicer's trust account every three months — [DRE RE 35 "Trust Deed Investments – What You Should Know"](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re35.pdf) (snippet). Form RE 852 (Trust Account Report, multi-lender) is triggered when multi-lender note payments exceed $125,000 in three months or 120 investors receive disbursements — [RE 852 copy](https://journal.firsttuesday.us/wp-content/uploads/delightful-downloads/546.pdf) (snippet). Doss Law adds that after the one-time RE 860 notice the broker files quarterly trust fund reports — [Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/alternatives-to-the-multi-lender-rule/) (snippet). **Unconfirmed:** whether the CPA review is quarterly or annual, and which trigger applies.
- Enforcement example: a DRE accusation against Trust Deed Services, Inc. cites failures to meet the threshold and multi-lender criteria and to file quarterly multi-lender reports — [DRE H35325LA](https://www2.dre.ca.gov/hearingfiles/H35325LA_P.pdf) (snippet)
- LTV caps (§10232.3): 80% improved, 50% unimproved, 65% single-family lot — [DRE RE 7](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true) (snippet)
- Investor disclosure (§§10232.4/10232.5 per secondary sources): RE 851A, 851B and 851D must be delivered and signed before investor funds are accepted — [Dan Harkey](https://danharkey.com/posts/deed-of-trust-investments-in-california-legal-framework-and-licensing-requirements); [Realty411](https://realty411.com/deed-of-trusts-and-mortgage-investments/) (snippets). The sources disagree on the exact section numbers.

**Article 6: multi-lender loans (§10237, §10238)**
- Fractionalized loans are exempt from qualification under the Corporate Securities Law of 1968 only if they have at most 10 lenders or note purchasers, each meeting net-worth or income tests. Self-dealing is barred except in narrow statutory cases. Servicing must be done by a real estate broker or other authorized party under a servicing agreement — [DRE Reference Book ch. 10](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/refbook/ref10.pdf) (snippet)
- The broker files the RE 860 notice within 30 days after its first multi-lender transaction — [DRE RE 860](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/forms/re860.pdf); [DRE RE 7](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf) (snippets). The DRE publishes a public list of multi-lender and threshold brokers — [DRE Multi-Lender/Threshold Brokers lookup](https://secure.dre.ca.gov/PublicASP/MultiLenderThresholdBrokers.asp)

**Article 7: Mortgage Loan Disclosure Statement (RE 882/883/885) and the "business-purpose exemption" question**
- The DRE's MLDS advisory says the MLDS (RE 882, 883 or 885) is required under §10240 from a broker to a borrower, and covers a loan on any type of real property, including commercial property and raw land. It must be delivered within 3 business days of receiving the written application, or before the borrower becomes obligated, whichever is earlier — [DRE MLDS and Loan Estimate Advisory (2015)](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/adv/2015_2019/Advisory2015MLDS%20and%20Loan%20Estimate%20Advisory.pdf) (snippet). RE 882 is the traditional form and RE 885 the nontraditional form — [DRE MLB forms](https://dre.ca.gov/Forms/MLB.html) (snippet)
- §10245 exempts most of Article 7 for bona fide loans secured by a first trust deed of $30,000 or more, or a junior lien of $20,000 or more. §§10240, 10240.3, 10242.5 and 10242.6 are excluded from that exemption, so §10240 (the MLDS) still applies — [FindLaw BPC §10245](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-10245/); [Justia §10245](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-7/section-10245/) (snippets)
- The broker keeps the signed MLDS acknowledgment for 3 years — [Justia §10240](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-7/section-10240/) (snippet)

**Trust accounts (10 CCR §2830 et seq.) and servicing (§10233)**
- §2832: funds the broker accepts for others must reach the owner, a neutral escrow or the broker's trust account within 3 business days of receipt — [Cornell LII 10 CCR §2832](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2832) (snippet)
- §2832.1: the broker needs written consent from each owner before any disbursement that would drop the balance below the aggregate owed — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2832) (snippet)
- §2834: who may sign trust-account withdrawals (the broker, or authorized licensed or bonded persons). The broker remains responsible — [Cornell LII 10 CCR §2834](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2834) (snippet)
- §2830: the account must be in the broker's name as trustee, at a financial institution. The broker may not accept inducements for placing the account without the client's written permission — [Cornell LII 10 CCR §2830](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2830); [DRE RE 13 Trust Funds](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re13.pdf) (snippets)
- §10233 covers a licensee who undertakes to service a promissory note. I could not retrieve its operative text — [LawServer Article 5 index](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/california/codes/california_business_professions_code_division_4_part_1_chapter_3_article_5) (snippet)

**Usury: Civil Code §1916.1 and its limits**
- §1916.1 exempts from the usury cap loans secured by real property that are made or arranged by a licensed real estate broker. Courts require that the broker was compensated, or expected compensation, and actually arranged the loan rather than appearing in name only — [White & Bright](https://www.whiteandbright.com/california-usury-law-exemptions-for-real-estate-brokers-2/); [Doss Law usury guide](https://www.dosslaw.com/definitive-guides/doss-law-llps-definitive-guide-to-usury-in-california/); [Note Servicing Center](https://noteservicingcenter.com/california-usury-mistakes-void-private-loan/) (snippets)
- *In re Moon* (Bankr. N.D. Cal. / BAP 2023) held that modifying a previously exempt broker-arranged loan held by an unlicensed lender could make it usurious, even if the rate was lowered. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished decision — [CLA case note](https://calawyers.org/business-law/in-re-moon-bankr-n-d-cal/); [GoDocs update](https://godocs.com/in-re-moon-update-forthcoming-changes-to-california-civil-code-1916-1/) (snippets)
- **2024–2025 change:** SB 1146 was sent to the Governor on 2024-08-28 and took effect 2025-01-01. It amends §1916.1 to cover "any loan, or any forbearance, extension, or modification of a loan, made or arranged by any person licensed as a real estate broker" — [GoDocs](https://godocs.com/in-re-moon-update-forthcoming-changes-to-california-civil-code-1916-1/); [Justia 2024 Civ. Code §1916.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-4/chapter-3/section-1916-1/) (snippets)
- **Residual risk:** the California Mortgage Association warns that the fix works only if a DRE broker actually negotiates or arranges the modification, forbearance or extension. One negotiated by an unlicensed servicer may still be exposed — [CMA "Ongoing Risks When Modifying Loans Post-SB 1146" (2025-06-05)](https://www.californiamortgageassociation.org/2025/06/05/ongoing-risks-when-modifying-loans-post-sb-1146/) (snippet)
- Doss Law says brokers who lend their own funds and hold the loan are not affected by *Moon* when they modify it. This is a practitioner's view, not a holding — [Doss Law usury guide](https://www.dosslaw.com/definitive-guides/doss-law-llps-definitive-guide-to-usury-in-california/) (snippet)
- A usury exemption does not remove the lender's licensing obligation — [calcorporatelaw.com](https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/2010/10/license-we-dont-need-no-stinking-license) (snippet, via a 2016 commentary in the same search)

**Securities law: fractional interests and funds**
- California exemptions commonly cited for fractional trust deeds are Corp. Code §25102(e), §25102(f) and §25102.5. One practitioner says §25102.5 and §25102(e) cap a deal at 10 investors with California property and California investors. §25102(f) allows up to 35 non-accredited purchasers plus unlimited accredited purchasers, with a pre-existing-relationship test and a notice filing. **The practitioner's own posts are internally inconsistent** — [Dan Harkey](https://www.danharkey.com/post/deed-of-trusts-and-mortgage-investments-in-california); [Justia Corp. Code §25102](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-corp/title-4/division-1/part-2/chapter-1/section-25102/) (snippets)
- Broker-dealer: Corp. Code §25206 exempts DRE brokers from §25210 only for interests in partnerships, LLCs and similar entities formed solely to invest in real property. A 1979 Commissioner ruling treats trust-deed notes as personal property, so a fund investing in notes reportedly cannot rely on §25206. Commissioner's Rule 260.204.1 gives a narrower additional exemption that requires notice to the Commissioner. None of this affects federal broker-dealer registration under the 1934 Act — [calcorporatelaw.com "Does George Babbitt Need a Broker-Dealer License"](https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/2010/08/does-george-babbitt-need-a-broker-dealer-license-part-1); [Justia 10 CCR 260.204.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-10/chapter-3/subchapter-2/article-8/section-260-204-1) (snippets; 2010)
- Federal Investment Company Act: §3(c)(5)(C) excludes companies primarily engaged in acquiring mortgages and other liens on real estate. The SEC's 2011 concept release questioned whether some mortgage pools resemble closed-end funds — [SEC concept release](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2011/08/companies-engaged-business-acquiring-mortgages-mortgage-related-instruments) (snippet). Example: a California mortgage fund's Form D lists §3(c)(5) as its exclusion — [Hall Mortgage Fund Form D/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1216652/000110465921059417/0001104659-21-059417-index.html). The DFPI has brought enforcement actions against California mortgage funds; the charges were not shown in the snippet — [DFPI: California Investment Mortgage Fund I LP](https://dfpi.ca.gov/enf-c/california-investment-mortgage-fund-i-lp/); [DFPI: CMR Mortgage Fund III](https://dfpi.ca.gov/enforcement_action/cmr-mortgage-fund-iii/)

**Other California rules checked**
- Commercial financing disclosure law (SB 1235; Fin. Code Div. 9.5, §22800 et seq.) exempts commercial financing transactions secured by real property under §22801. CMA flagged a gap for mezzanine or equity-pledge structures — [Senate Judiciary analysis](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/media/1671); [CMA letter](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/04/PRO-01-18-California-Mortgage-Association.pdf); [Buchalter on final DFPI regs, June 2022](https://www.buchalter.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/California-DFPI-Publishes-Final-Commercial-Loan-Disclosure-Regulations.Richards.pdf) (snippets)
- Foreclosure: SB 1079 (2020, effective 2021) and Civ. Code §2924m apply to properties with 1–4 residential units. They ban bundling and give eligible tenants and bidders a post-sale right to buy. AB 1837 (2022) and AB 1957 (2024, chaptered 2024-07-02) amended the regime. Several sources said it would sunset January 1, 2026; **I could not confirm whether it was extended.** It does not apply to commercial collateral, but it does apply to 1–4-unit fix-and-flip collateral — [AAPL on SB 1079](https://aaplonline.com/articles/news/california-passes-sb-1079-creating-right-of-first-refusal-after-trustee-sales/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/sb-1079-changes-to-nonjudicial-foreclosure-process-aim-to-benefit-tenants-primary); [Senate Judiciary AB 1837 analysis](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/sites/sjud.senate.ca.gov/files/ab_1837_mia_bonta_sjud_analysis.pdf); [United Trustees Assn on AB 1957](https://unitedtrustees.com/?p=13444) (snippets)

### Inferences
- **"Business-purpose loans are exempt from the MLDS" is not supported.** The DRE's own advisory says the MLDS applies to commercial property and raw land, and §10245's large-loan exemption carves §10240 back in. Kubera should assume an RE 882 is required on every loan it negotiates as broker unless counsel identifies a specific exemption, such as a lender-side or institutional exemption the DRE manual alludes to. This contradicts the framing in the brief and is the most important correction in this section.
- **Usury:** Kubera's "usury-exempt under §1916.1" claim holds only where Kubera actually arranges the loan and is compensated or expects to be, or makes it with its own funds. After SB 1146, a licensed DRE broker, preferably Kubera, should arrange any extension or modification. Kubera's platform should log broker involvement and compensation on every modification as usury evidence.
- **Licensing paths:** (a) stay DRE-only, arranging and table funding with the broker making or arranging loans under §1916.1 and §22050; or (b) add a CFL license for a Kubera lending entity that funds from its own balance sheet. A CFL licensee is usury-exempt under §22002 but reportedly cannot use trust-deed investors under that license. If Kubera wants to sell notes to individuals, the DRE broker license plus Article 5 and Article 6 compliance is the natural vehicle.
- **Threshold status:** Kubera would likely become a threshold broker quickly at $750k–$25M ticket sizes; 10 loans at about $1M would cross the $1,000,000 aggregate on any reading. Plan for RE 853 notice, RE 881 annual reports, trust-fund reporting and a CPA review from the start.

### Gaps
- I could not open statute text for §§10131, 10232, 10232.2, 10232.25, 10233, 10237, 10238, 10240 or Civ. Code §1916.1 because leginfo and dre.ca.gov are blocked here. The threshold criteria, the quarterly vs annual reporting cadence, CPA-review triggers, §10238's investor qualification tests, and whether §10232.3's LTV caps can be exceeded with written justification all need verification.
- Whether a Kubera-affiliated fund lending on loans Kubera arranges is covered by §1916.1 and §22050, given self-dealing and affiliate concerns, was not answered. Counsel must decide.
- AB 2116 (2025–26) content and status, and any 2025–2026 DRE regulation changes to RE 851A/B or threshold-broker reporting, were not found.
- Whether §2924m's sunset (2026-01-01) was extended is unknown.

## 4. Servicing: who services after funding, and what servicing involves

### Takeaway
After funding, the note is serviced either in-house by the broker (the activity §10233 addresses, with trust-account and multi-lender reporting duties) or by a licensed third-party subservicer such as FCI Lender Services. Servicing covers payment collection, impounds, investor remittances, default management through California non-judicial trustee sales, and 1098/1099 reporting. Published servicing fee schedules were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-lender deals must provide for servicing by a real estate broker or other authorized party under a servicing agreement — [DRE Reference Book ch. 10](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/refbook/ref10.pdf) (snippet). Multi-lender servicing agreements must require a CPA inspection of the servicer's trust account every three months — [DRE RE 35](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re35.pdf) (snippet)
- FCI Lender Services acts as a subservicer at the direction of the lender client (the note owner). Its standard program covers performing and newly originated private money loans; a separate "Full Collection" program covers loans that have missed a third payment. Its site does not publish prices. A $150 loss-mitigation response fee applies outside Full Collection — [FCI loan servicing page](https://trustfci.com/LoanServicing.html) (snippet). Trade press calls FCI the nation's largest "private money" servicer — [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/nations-largest-private-money-servicer-showing-strong-growth) (headline; date not confirmed). A BiggerPockets post mentions a $30/month escrow-related fee; it is undated and unverified — [BiggerPockets](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/70/topics/162758-which-loan-servicer-no-longer-like-trustfcicom)
- Some trust-deed marketplaces pay subservicer fees out of their servicing spread rather than charging investors — [Private Lender Link profile](https://privatelenderlink.com/profile/residential-first-capital/) (snippet; one firm's self-description)
- Servicing-software functions, as vendors describe them:
  - TMO: payment application notices, statement distribution, ACH deposits, AutoPay debits, and support for Forms 1098 and 1099 — [TMO private lending](https://www.themortgageoffice.com/industries/private-lending/); [TMO multiple loan types](https://www.themortgageoffice.com/how-the-mortgage-office-supports-lenders-managing-multiple-loan-types) (snippets)
  - Baseline: pre-authorized debits, a borrower payment portal and servicing reports — [Private Lender Link on Baseline](https://privatelenderlink.com/2024/02/baseline-software-features-and-experience-for-private-lenders/) (snippet, Feb 2024)

### Inferences
- **Recommended for Kubera:** outsource servicing at launch to a licensed California subservicer (FCI or a peer) that already supports multi-lender remittances, 1098/1099 reporting and trustee-sale coordination. Kubera keeps the broker-of-record role and approves any modification or extension itself, to preserve §1916.1 coverage after SB 1146. Revisit in-house servicing once volume justifies CPA-reviewed trust accounting.
- **California non-judicial foreclosure:** default management means a notice of default, a statutory waiting period, a notice of sale, then a trustee's sale. I did not verify the specific day-count timelines (Civ. Code §2924 et seq.) in this session, so they are not stated here. Counsel or the trustee must confirm them.

### Gaps
- No published fee schedules found for FCI, Trust Deed Investment Services or other California subservicers. Searches for the named servicers returned nothing usable.
- Statutory text of §10233 not retrieved.
- California non-judicial foreclosure timelines were not verified in this session.

## 6. What Kubera Commercial is missing to run hard money end to end (build / integrate / skip)

### Takeaway
Kubera's stack already covers intake, AI term sheets, routing, CRM, documents, e-sign, workflows and approval gates; that is roughly the origination front end. To run hard money end to end it still lacks hard-money sizing and underwriting objects, California-specific broker and investor compliance artifacts, draw management, the investor side (onboarding, allocation, portal, distributions), trust accounting, servicing and default management. Build what is differentiating and close to Kubera's AI pipeline. Integrate the regulated ledgers (trust and fund accounting, servicing, 1098/1099). Skip a full in-house servicing operation and a pooled fund until volume and counsel justify them.

### Cited Findings
- The required California artifacts these capabilities map to:
  - RE 882/885 MLDS on all property types, delivered within 3 business days — [DRE MLDS advisory](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/adv/2015_2019/Advisory2015MLDS%20and%20Loan%20Estimate%20Advisory.pdf)
  - RE 851A, 851B and 851D signed before investor funds are accepted — [DRE MLB forms](https://dre.ca.gov/Forms/MLB.html)
  - RE 870 investor questionnaire, with 4-year retention — [DRE RE 7](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true)
  - RE 853 threshold notice and RE 860 multi-lender notice — [DRE MLB forms](https://www.dre.ca.gov/forms/mlb.html); [RE 860](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/forms/re860.pdf)
  - RE 881 annual report within 90 days of fiscal year-end — [RE 881 instructions](https://bars.dre.ca.gov/pdfs/re881_Instructions.pdf)
  - Trust-fund deposit within 3 business days — [10 CCR §2832](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2832)
  - Exemption declaration in the loan file — [Justia §10236.7](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-5/section-10236-7/)
  - Broker involvement in any modification, for §1916.1 coverage after SB 1146 — [CMA 2025](https://www.californiamortgageassociation.org/2025/06/05/ongoing-risks-when-modifying-loans-post-sb-1146/)

### Inferences
These tags are recommendations derived from the findings above, not sourced facts.

**Origination and underwriting**
1. **Hard money sizing in the loan sizer and Jev rules** (as-is LTV, LTARV, LTC, interest reserve, rehab holdback, extension options, default rate, minimum interest, exit fee), plus California LTV-cap checks (80% improved, 50% unimproved, 65% lot) whenever investor money is used. **BUILD.**
2. **Hard money underwriting objects:** ARV and valuation record, budget and scope of work with line items, borrower track-record schedule (tiering), exit-strategy analysis (takeout DSCR or sale comps), feasibility stress test. Fits naturally into the planned document-extraction and ratios agent. **BUILD.**
