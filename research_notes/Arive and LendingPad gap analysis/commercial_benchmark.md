## Q1. Which platforms do commercial mortgage brokers, CRE debt advisors and small commercial lenders use for deal management, lender outreach and closing?

### Takeaway
No single commercial equivalent of Arive or LendingPad exists for brokers. One 2026 guide says the typical broker stack pairs a generic or CRE-specific CRM with a CRE debt tool for lender matching, packaging and distribution. Lev is the most complete broker-side product (CRM, AI lender search, deal checklist and vault, OM generation, quote matrix). Janover Pro and CommLoan are the main lender-matching and distribution alternatives. Lender-side systems (nCino, Abrigo, Lenders Cooperative/Ventures, Built) are bank-grade LOS and servicing platforms that a broker sends work to, not tools a broker would run.

### Inferences
- The broker-side commercial market splits into three layers, and none does LOS-style loan file management end to end:
  - CRM: Apto, ClientLook, AscendixRE, or the CRM inside Lev
  - Lender matching and distribution: Lev, Janover Pro, CommLoan, Finance Lobby
  - Extraction and spreading: Clik.ai, Blooma and similar
- A residential LOS's core features do not translate to these brokers: URLA/1003, AUS (DU/LP), pricing engines, TRID/LE/CD disclosures and HMDA. The commercial equivalents are lender matching against credit boxes, OM and loan package generation, and quote-matrix comparison.
- Corin's verticals (HUD 232, SBA, hotel CMBS, PIP bridge) are mostly served by lender-side tools, not broker-side ones. Lev and Janover appear tuned to conventional CRE debt; HUD 232 and SBA specialty workflows are not mentioned in any snippet for them.

## Q2. What capabilities recur across these platforms?

### Takeaway
Six capabilities recur across commercial broker and debt platforms:
1. Lender database with credit-box matching
2. Multi-lender distribution with response tracking
3. Quote and term-sheet comparison matrices
4. AI-generated OMs and deal books
5. Deal checklists and document vaults shared with borrowers and lenders
6. CRM with email logging

Financial spreading (T-12, rent roll, DSCR) is a separate tool category. SBA forms, E-Tran, KYC/OFAC and credit pulls belong to lender-side LOS, not broker platforms.

### Inferences
- **Residential LOS features that translate to commercial brokerage:**
  - Pipeline and stages
  - Borrower portal and document checklist (needs per-program checklists, e.g. HUD 232 exhibits, SBA package, CMBS hotel package)
  - Conditions and closing tracker
  - CRM sync and email/SMS
  - E-sign
  - Compensation tracking (in commercial: broker-fee agreements and invoices, not LO comp plans)
  - Audit trail
- **Commercial-specific features residential LOS lack:**
  - Credit-box lender database and matching
  - Multi-lender distribution with response tracking
  - Term-sheet parsing into a quote matrix
  - OM and loan package generation
  - Property-level spreading (T-12, rent roll, STR reports for hotels, census and payor mix for senior living)
  - DSCR, debt yield and LTV sizing
  - Sponsor and guarantor schedules (REO, PFS)
  - Third-party report (appraisal, Phase I, PCNA/PCA) order tracking
  - SBA Form 159 fee disclosure
  - Program-specific exhibit checklists
- **Residential features that do not translate:** URLA/1003, AUS, residential PPE, TRID disclosures, HMDA and the CA DRE residential disclosures.

### Gaps
- I found no broker-side product that orders or tracks appraisals, Phase Is or PCNAs; the LightBox, Collateral360 and ValuTrac search returned nothing relevant.
- I found no broker-side product with fee invoicing or broker-fee agreement workflow.
- I found no snippet confirming DSCR or debt-yield calculation inside Lev, Janover or nCino. "Underwriting calculators" at Janover is unspecified.
- I found no product that pulls guarantor credit for brokers; not researched to a source.

## Q3. Which SBA and HUD forms, checklists and submission steps would a broker prepare for the lender, and does software automate them?

### Takeaway
For SBA deals, a broker typically prepares the borrower's package:
- Form 1919
- Form 413 PFS or the lender's own form
- Financials and eligibility information
- **SBA Form 159 fee disclosure**: the broker must sign it whenever it is paid as an "agent", making it the key broker-specific compliance item

The lender, not the broker, submits through E-Tran. Lender LOS (Abrigo, nCino, Lenders Cooperative and others) automate 1919/1920 generation and E-Tran.

For HUD 232/223(f), only an FHA- and MAP-approved lender with a MAP-approved healthcare underwriter can submit to ORCF, so the broker's role is assembling borrower and operator exhibits:
- HUD-2530 previous participation, now via APPS
- Financials
- Facility narrative

I found no software that automates HUD 232 packaging.

Abrigo's page still advertises generating 1919 and 1920 PDFs — [Abrigo](https://www.abrigo.com/software/lending-and-credit-risk/small-business-lending-software/sba-lending/).   - Violations "may result in SBA's suspension or revocation of the privilege of conducting business with the SBA" — [Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/colemans-sba-compliance-report-know-the-new-form-159-rules/)

- **SOP:** SOP 50 10 8 is effective June 1, 2025 — [Coleman Report copy of SOP 50 10 8](https://colemanreport.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/SOP-50-10-8-effective-6.1.2025-Final.pdf)

**HUD 232/223(f)**

  - ORCF handles Section 232. Only FHA-approved lenders that are MAP-approved, with a MAP-approved healthcare underwriter, can process the files — [HUD ORCF](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/healthcare-programs-orcf)
  - The lender assembles exhibits, signs certifications and submits to ORCF — [hud.loans lender marketing, via snippet](https://www.hud.loans/fha-232-223f/)
  - HUD's ORCF page lists a "232 Healthcare Portal" with lender access forms — [HUD ORCF](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/healthcare-programs-orcf)

- **223(f) certification:** the lender must certify the application complies with Section 223(f), with supporting documents dated before submission. This comes from an older handbook redline — [HUD Section 232 Handbook Ch. 2 redline](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/documents/Ch02_Gen_Prg_Reqs__Rev2_DT2_Final_Redline.pdf)
- **Previous participation (HUD-2530):**
  - Each principal files a HUD-2530 at initial application; older handbooks also list HUD-92013 and HUD-92264 as required forms — [HUD handbook](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/45611c5hsgh.pdf)
  - APPS is an electronic alternative to the paper 2530 — [OMB 2022 ICR](https://omb.report/icr/202211-2502-005/doc/126965300.pdf)
  - A 2017 HUD email blast says the paper HUD-2530 is no longer accepted for ORCF projects (ORCF is the Section 232 office) — [HUD email blast May 2017](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/documents/Email-Blast-2008-May2017.pdf)
- **Facility requirement:** HUD requires a formal software-based incident-reporting and risk-management system at each 232-insured facility. This is an operator requirement worth flagging to borrowers — [hud232.loan, via snippet](https://www.hud232.loan/hud-232-lean-processing/)
- **Timeline:** 232 loans typically take 6 to 8 months from application to close (lender marketing) — [multifamily.loans](https://www.multifamily.loans/hud-232-lean-loans/)
- **Audited financials:** 232 refinancing requires audited financial statements — [Pease Bell CPAs](https://www.peasebell.com/insights/hud-232-refinancing/)

### Inferences
- For SBA, Corin's highest-value broker automation is not E-Tran, which is the lender's job. It is:
  1. Pre-filling 1919 and the PFS from intake data
  2. Collecting and indexing the standard package (tax returns, interim financials, debt schedule, resumes, affiliate list)
  3. Generating and e-signing Form 159 with the correct fee amount
  4. Running an eligibility pre-check against SOP 50 10 8 (size, use of proceeds, ineligible businesses, citizenship/ownership)
- For HUD 232, the broker's value is assembling operator and borrower exhibits for the HUD lender's underwriter:
  - Facility narrative
  - 3 years of operating statements plus T-12
  - Census and payor mix

## Q5. Which commercial capabilities is Corin Commercial missing (today and after its planned build)?

### Takeaway
After its planned build, Corin covers most commercial broker workflow: pipeline, lender matching, OM generation, quote comparison, document and closing agents, vendor tracking, portals and consent. Gaps that remain:
- **Not on the plan at all:**
  - SBA Form 159 and other SBA-specific package automation
  - Program-specific exhibit checklists (HUD 232, SBA, CMBS hotel)
  - KYC/OFAC/PEP screening of borrowers and guarantors
  - Guarantor credit authorization and pulls
  - Broker-fee agreements, invoicing and commission tracking
  - Sponsor REO and PFS schedules
  - Lender outreach and response tracking as a distinct object
  - A licensed external lender-universe dataset
- **Planned but should be prioritized:** DSCR, debt-yield and global-cash-flow spreading on extracted data, and an audit trail.

### Cited Findings (the benchmark each gap is measured against)
- **Lender outreach and response tracking:** Janover Pro markets "multi-lender deal distribution and response tracking" — [Janover guide](https://janover.pro/guides/best-crm-for-commercial-mortgage-brokers). Finance Lobby has lenders "indicate interest" on a published deal — [Finance Lobby](https://lp.financelobby.com/CRElendingmarketplace). Corin's plan has matching and term-sheet parsing but no explicit distribution and response log per lender.
- **External lender universe:** Lev claims 7,000+ capital sources — [Lev](https://www.lev.com/stakeholders/brokers); CommLoan 900+ lenders and 600k programs — [NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/commloan-launches-ai-powered-lender-matching-tool-cre-mortgage-brokers). Corin matches only against a static list plus self-registered credit boxes.
- **Quote matrix:** Lev's quote matrix compares rate, LTV and recourse — [Lev finance](https://www.lev.com/finance). Corin's planned term-sheet parsing matches this; it should also normalize all-in cost — [AI Consulting Network](https://www.theaiconsultingnetwork.com/blog/ai-cre-lender-term-sheet-comparison-debt-quotes-2026).
- **AI checklists:** Lev's AI checklist is generated from loan type — [Lev changelog](https://www.lev.com/docs/changelog/week-of-2026-04-19). Corin's document-chasing agent should be driven by per-program templates.
- **Form 159:** brokers paid as agents must sign it, with penalties for non-compliance — [SBA Form 159](https://www.sba7a.loans/s/SBA-form-159-official.pdf); [Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/colemans-sba-compliance-report-know-the-new-form-159-rules/). It is not in Corin's plan.
- **1919 and PFS:** borrowers complete 1919 for the lender — [FitSmallBusiness](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/sba-form-1919-borrower-information/). Lenders' LOS accept 1919 data, e.g. nCino via DocuSign — [nCino SBA](https://www.ncino.com/sba). Corin could pre-fill these.
- **HUD-2530/APPS:** principals' previous-participation data is required for 232 — [HUD handbook](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/45611c5hsgh.pdf); [HUD 2017 blast](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/documents/Email-Blast-2008-May2017.pdf). Corin's sponsor and principal intake should collect it.
- **Spreading:** T-12 and rent-roll extraction is a mature, cheap category — [Docsumo](https://www.docsumo.com/blog/best-cre-automation-tools). Corin's planned AI document agent covers extraction; the gap is the spread and ratio layer (NOI normalization, DSCR at each lender's constant, debt yield, global cash flow across guarantors). This matters for SBA because lenders run global cash flow — [CCG Catalyst](https://www.ccgcatalyst.com/thought-leadership/research-snapshot/sector-spotlight-sba-7a-underwriting-support-technology-for-loan-origination/).
- **KYC/OFAC:** available via inexpensive per-check APIs — [ComplyCube](https://complycube.com/pricing). It is not in Corin's plan.

### Inferences
Prioritized gap list (my judgment):
1. **Lender outreach log:** per-deal, per-lender status (sent, NDA, reviewing, quoted, declined with reason), linked to Twenty opportunities. Cheap, and the core of debt-broker work.
2. **Program checklist templates:** HUD 232/223(f) exhibits, SBA 7(a) and 504 packages, hotel CMBS/PIP (STR, PIP scope, franchise comfort letter), and agency multifamily. These drive the document-chasing and closing agents.
3. **Broker-fee agreement, invoicing and commission ledger:** includes SBA Form 159 generation and e-sign, and splits with referral brokers already captured in partner registration.
4. **Spreading layer:** turns extracted T-12, rent roll, STR and census data into DSCR, debt yield and LTV per lender quote; global cash flow for SBA; feeds the loan sizer and OM.
5. **Sponsor and guarantor module:** REO schedule, PFS (413-style), HUD-2530/APPS principal data, org chart, and credit-authorization capture.
6. **KYC/OFAC/PEP screening** of borrowers, guarantors and key principals via API, logged into the planned consent and audit ledger.
7. **Licensed lender data** (e.g. Lev) to widen matching beyond registered credit boxes. Optional.
8. **Audit trail** across staff approvals, AI outputs and document access. Partly covered by the planned AI conversation logs and consent ledger; should extend to every status change.

Already covered or planned, matching the benchmark:
- Loan sizer and fee breakdown
- Lead classification and routing (Jev)
- AI indicative term sheet
- OM generation
- Term-sheet parsing
- Document vault, portals and e-sign
- Vendor order tracking
- CRM sync

Corin's AI advisor and urgency-tiered routing go beyond the snippet-level features I found at Lev, Janover or CommLoan. That is my inference from the absence of such features in the snippets, not a verified comparison.
