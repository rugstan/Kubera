# Skip the 1003, build the deal desk

Most of what Arive and LendingPad sell has no use for Kubera Commercial. Roughly half of each platform is residential consumer-mortgage machinery: TRID disclosures, DU/LPA automated underwriting, HMDA and NMLS call reports, MERS, rate-sheet pricing engines and the 1003/MISMO loan file. None of it applies to a California DRE-licensed broker arranging business-purpose HUD, SBA, CMBS and bridge debt. The other half is workflow scaffolding: auto-advancing milestones, role-scoped pipelines, a condition loop, a locked document vault, e-sign and compensation fields. Kubera's roadmap already plans most of it. The real gaps come from comparing Kubera with commercial broker tools (Lev, Janover Pro, CommLoan, Finance Lobby) and with the program rules behind Kubera's niches. Kubera's plan has **no per-lender outreach and response log**, **no program-specific exhibit checklists** for HUD 232/223(f), SBA and hotel CMBS/PIP, **no fee and commission ledger** (and with it no SBA Form 159, the broker's main SBA compliance document), **no spreading layer** that turns extracted T-12, STR and census data into DSCR, debt yield and global cash flow, **no sponsor/guarantor module**, and **no KYC/OFAC screening**. No vendor sells broker-side tooling for HUD 232 or SBA packaging, so Kubera should build nearly all of the workflow itself. It should integrate only commodity services (e-sign, sanctions screening, optionally a licensed lender universe) and skip the entire residential compliance stack. A prioritized build / integrate / skip list closes the report.

A caveat on sources: the research team could not open arive.com, lendingpad.com, lev.com or janover.pro directly. Every vendor fact below comes from search-result snippets of the cited pages. Kubera's "has" and "planned" status comes from the internal project brief, not from inspecting the codebase.

## Half of each residential platform is compliance Kubera never touches

Arive is a San Ramon, CA all-in-one platform for independent residential brokers. It combines an LOS, a borrower point of sale, a product and pricing engine and an integrated wholesale-lender marketplace ([Arive support](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000271217-what-is-arive-)). Its 2026 activity is almost entirely lender onboarding: HomeXpress, Loanstream, LendingPros, RISE TPO, GIANT, Keystone, GMFS, TPO Go and others joined the marketplace between January and July ([Arive press, GMFS](https://www.arive.com/press/gmfs-joins-the-arive-platform-to-deliver-faster-pricing-streamlined-workflows-and-high-touch-partner-support)). That marketplace is the moat. HomeXpress says about **33% of its active broker partners already run on Arive** ([HomeXpress PR](https://homexmortgage.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/HomeXpress-_-ARIVE-PR-02.26.26.pdf)). LendingPad, from WEI Technology in McLean, VA, sells a per-user Broker Edition and a per-closed-loan Lender Edition ([MPA Mag](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-broker-software/lendingpad-review/445438)). Its distinctive asset is the same idea built differently: a "Register with Lender" button that clones the broker's file into a LendingPad-hosted wholesaler's pipeline ([LendingPad KB](https://kb.lendingpad.com/lendingpad-brokers-operating-with-lendingpad-wholesale-lenders)).

Strip away the lender network and what remains is mostly consumer-mortgage compliance. Arive generates the Loan Estimate, change-of-circumstance forms, Closing Disclosure and closing package through DocMagic ([Arive support](https://support.arive.com/en/support/solutions/articles/61000308107)). It runs DU and LPA, which require agency credentials and a priced residential product ([Arive: Run AUS](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000273604-run-aus-du-and-lpa-)), and it exports the NMLS Mortgage Call Report. Arive itself has **excluded business-purpose investment loans from that report since Q1 2024** ([Arive: MCR](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000281236-generate-call-reports-mcr-)). LendingPad auto-selects initial disclosure packages by loan type and state, and it supports HMDA export, MCR filing and DU/LPA ([LendingPad KB: Getting Started](https://kb.lendingpad.com/getting-started-in-lendingpad); [LendingPad KB: FAQs](https://kb.lendingpad.com/faqs)). These are consumer rules: TRID, HMDA (Reg C) and the loan-originator compensation rule all sit on the SAFE MLO exam outline ([NMLS test outline](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/testingHbk/education/mlo_testing/mlo_test_faq/mlo_testing_hbk_testOutline.html)). Kubera's loans are business-purpose credit, and Kubera is not the lender of record, so this whole layer drops out. Counsel should confirm it, but nothing in the notes points the other way.

Neither platform reaches Kubera's asset classes. Arive's furthest step toward "commercial" in 2026 was an automatic DSCR calculation and a **"Multi-Family (5–10 Units)" property type for non-QM** ([Arive v2026.2.0.0](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000317947-arive-release-notes-v2026-2-0-0)). LendingPad's business-purpose support means DSCR investor loans priced through LoanNEX's non-QM marketplace ([LoanNEX](https://loannex.com/blog/loannex-and-lendingpad-partner-to-create-the-most-comprehensive-access-to-non-qm-products/)). Gartner files LendingPad under "Commercial Loan Origination," but on a single review ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/lendingpad)). That is a taxonomy artifact, not a capability. The research found no Arive or LendingPad support for hotels, senior housing, 11+ unit multifamily, SBA, HUD or CMBS. Price is not the obstacle: Arive's Core seat lists at about **$49.99–$59.99 per user per month** ([Arive pricing](https://www.arive.com/pricing)) and LendingPad's Broker Edition at about **$40–$55** ([SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/lendingpad/pricing); [ZoftwareHub](https://zoftwarehub.com/products/lendingpad/pricing)). Fit is the obstacle. Both are built around a 1003 file and a single residential lender. Adopting either would mean fighting the data model on every commercial deal.

## The transferable half is workflow scaffolding, and Kubera has planned most of it

The useful lessons from both platforms are design patterns, not modules. LendingPad's milestones **advance automatically from dates entered in the file**, not by hand ([LendingPad KB: Getting Started](https://kb.lendingpad.com/getting-started-in-lendingpad)). Entering an "Application Taken" date moves a lead into the pipeline ([LendingPad KB: loan status](https://lendingpad.com/kb/how-to-change-a-loan-status-in-lendingpad)). Admins can also lock fields by status, for example "lock out LO's from editing 1003, Fee screens from Processing status and onward." Arive added task assignment in 2026 and task auto-completion in 2025 ([Arive v2026.1.0.0](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000317533-arive-release-notes-v2026-1-0-0); [Arive v2025.7.0.0](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000316064-arive-release-notes-v2025-7-0-0)). Since 2024 it has made finalized documents immutable: they cannot be reclassified, renamed, edited or deleted ([Arive v2024.5.0.0](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000312763-arive-release-notes-v2024-5-0-0)). Kubera's planned pipeline dashboard, staff login and closing-checklist agent cover the same ground in principle. They should explicitly adopt three of these patterns. Stages should advance from events, such as a term sheet received or a third-party report delivered. Key deal fields should lock once a term sheet is accepted. Documents should get a finalize-lock state backed by an append-only audit log.

The condition loop is the most transferable concept, and the residential platforms show how hard it is to automate across firms. Arive's conditions exchange is lender-specific. JMAC, for example, lets brokers view and submit conditions inside Arive ([JMAC help](https://help.jmaclending.com/hc/en-us/articles/50989320206099-Managing-Conditions-in-ARIVE)), and the research found no lender-agnostic tracker. LendingPad's April 2026 "Phase 1" lender integrations brought real-time condition syncing with UWM ([LendingPad press](https://blog.lendingpad.com/news-and-press-releases/lendingpad-releases-first-phase-of-lender-integrations-to-centralize-wholesale-and-investor-workflows)). Even so, UWM-sourced conditions cannot be edited or cleared in LendingPad, and **documents and statuses still do not flow back** ([LendingPad KB: UWM](https://kb.lendingpad.com/how-to-obtain-personal-api-keys-for-the-uwm-portal)). If two-way file exchange is still partial in standardized, MISMO-based residential lending, commercial lenders with bespoke commitment letters will not offer intake APIs. Kubera should build its own lender-agnostic conditions tracker. Each item records the condition text, owner, due date, linked documents and who cleared it. Conditions can be parsed from commitment letters and fed to the planned document-chasing agent. Delivery to lenders runs by email, signed data-room links and a lender view in the partner portal, not by API.

Compensation is where both residential platforms stop short. LendingPad stores lender-paid or borrower-paid broker comp and syncs it to the disclosure screen. Branches and broker records hold compensation amounts ([LendingPad KB: Settings](https://kb.lendingpad.com/settings)). Arive captures compensation date, amount and payer only to feed the call report ([Arive: MCR](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000281236-generate-call-reports-mcr-)). Neither tracks receivables, invoices or referral splits. Commercial broker tools do no better: the benchmark found **no broker-side product with fee invoicing or a broker-fee agreement workflow**. Kubera cannot buy this. It must build a deal-level fee ledger.

Two residential features are worth adopting in a different form. First, LendingPad's two-click "Register with Lender" handoff has a flaw: it locks the broker out of editing the file once it is sent ([LendingPad KB](https://kb.lendingpad.com/lendingpad-brokers-operating-with-lendingpad-wholesale-lenders)). Kubera should send versioned package links and keep editing rights. Second, Arive's Zapier-only integration story is a cautionary tale ([Arive: Zapier](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000312645-how-does-zapier-integration-work-)), and so is LendingPad's NDA-gated API, available only to Lender Edition clients ([APIs.io](https://apis.io/providers/lendingpad/)). Kubera's direct Supabase and Twenty integrations already beat both. Kubera is also ahead on AI. Arive's only AI item is a "Voice-to-Loan" announcement dated April 1, 2025, possibly a joke ([Arive press](https://www.arive.com/press/arive-unveils-voice-to-loan-transcoder-powered-by-generative-ai)). LendingPad says it is still "evaluating where AI could responsibly add value" ([LendingPad resources](https://lendingpad.com/resources/)).

## Commercial broker tools add a shop-the-deal layer Kubera only half-plans

Residential platforms assume one lender per file. Commercial brokerage is the opposite: one deal is packaged, shopped to many capital sources, and won on competing term sheets. No commercial Arive exists. A 2026 guide, self-interested because Janover published it, says brokers pair a generic CRM with a CRE debt tool for lender matching, packaging and distribution ([Janover Pro guide](https://janover.pro/guides/best-crm-for-commercial-mortgage-brokers)). Six capabilities recur across Lev, Janover Pro, CommLoan and Finance Lobby: a lender database with credit-box matching, multi-lender distribution with response tracking, quote matrices, AI-generated OMs, shared checklists and vaults, and CRM with email logging.

Kubera's plan covers four of the six. Lev's vault checklist turns a deal's documents into a collection workflow for borrower intake, lender due diligence and closing. Guests can upload and comment, and AI drafts the checklist from the deal description and loan type ([Lev changelog](https://www.lev.com/docs/changelog/week-of-2026-04-19)). Lev also generates AI deal books with an "Index" that keeps OM figures consistent with the deal data ([Lev changelog](https://lev.com/docs/changelog)), and it offers shareable quote matrices comparing rate, LTV and recourse ([Lev finance](https://www.lev.com/finance)). Kubera's planned OM generation, term-sheet parsing into quote comparison, borrower portal and Twenty CRM sync match these. One refinement matters: the quote matrix should normalize **all-in cost of capital** rather than headline rate, folding in fees, reserves, prepayment and recourse ([The AI Consulting Network](https://www.theaiconsultingnetwork.com/blog/ai-cre-lender-term-sheet-comparison-debt-quotes-2026)).

Two of the six are missing. The first is **distribution and response tracking as a first-class object**. Janover markets "multi-lender deal distribution and response tracking" ([Janover Pro guide](https://janover.pro/guides/best-crm-for-commercial-mortgage-brokers)). On Finance Lobby, a broker publishes a deal once and lenders indicate interest with soft quotes ([Finance Lobby](https://lp.financelobby.com/CRElendingmarketplace)). Kubera plans matching against registered credit boxes, but it has no per-deal, per-lender log recording sent date, package version, NDA status, reviewing, quoted, or declined with reason. That log is the core of debt-broker work. It is cheap to build on Supabase linked to Twenty opportunities. It also feeds the most useful management metric Kubera can produce: lender hit rate by program and asset type.

The second is **lender universe breadth**. Lev claims real-time data on **7,000+ capital sources** ([Lev brokers](https://www.lev.com/stakeholders/brokers)). CommLoan's CUPID engine matches deals against **600,000 loan programs from 900+ lenders** and returns ranked matches in under a minute, and its quote generator is free for brokers ([National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/commloan-launches-ai-powered-lender-matching-tool-cre-mortgage-brokers)). Kubera matches only against a static list plus self-registered credit boxes. It cannot replicate a 7,000-source dataset. Its own credit-box registry remains a sound differentiator because Lev and Janover market the same mechanism ([Janover](https://janover.co/)). A licensed feed is an optional supplement, not a build target.

## Kubera's niche programs are served by no broker software at all

Lev and Janover appear tuned to conventional CRE debt. None of the snippets for them mention HUD 232 or SBA workflows. The software that does handle those programs belongs to lenders. Abrigo generates SBA forms and connects to E-Tran ([Abrigo](https://www.abrigo.com/software/lending-and-credit-risk/small-business-lending-software/sba-lending/)). nCino supports Form 1919 through DocuSign ([nCino SBA](https://www.ncino.com/sba)). Lenders Cooperative, which merged with Ventures in February 2025, claims a role in processing **99.8% of SBA 504 loans in 2024** ([BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20250213176154/en/Lenders-Cooperative-and-Ventures-Lending-Technologies-Complete-Merger-Creating-a-Unified-End-to-End-Lending-and-Servicing-Solution)). Kubera should buy none of these. E-Tran is the lender's job, because lenders submit guaranty requests through SBA's systems ([SBA lenders](https://sba.gov/sba-lenders)). For HUD 232, only an FHA-approved, MAP-approved lender with a MAP-approved healthcare underwriter can submit to ORCF ([HUD ORCF](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/healthcare-programs-orcf)). The broker's value is assembling the package those systems ingest. The research found **no software anywhere that automates HUD 232 packaging**.

For SBA, one broker obligation stands out. Form 159, the Fee Disclosure and Compensation Agreement, defines an "agent" to include a packager, consultant or broker. It must be completed whenever the applicant or lender pays an agent, and when the applicant pays, the agent, applicant and lender all sign ([SBA Form 159](https://www.sba7a.loans/s/SBA-form-159-official.pdf); [FitSmallBusiness](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/sba-form-159/)). Fees cannot be paid by both applicant and lender for the same service ([Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/sba-loan-packaging-and-referral-fees-know-the-new-rules/)). Violations can bring **suspension or revocation of the privilege of doing business with SBA** ([Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/colemans-sba-compliance-report-know-the-new-form-159-rules/)). The latest edition found is 08-21, with an OMB expiration of 11/30/2024, so Kubera must confirm the current version before templating it ([MidPenn copy](https://midpennbank.com/content/uploads/2021/11/SBA-Form-159-updated_508-FINAL-11.22.2021.pdf)). Form 159 belongs on top of the fee ledger: the ledger knows the fee amount, payer and agent, so it can generate the form and route it for signature. Kubera can also prefill borrower-side forms from intake. The applicant completes Form 1919 for the lender ([FitSmallBusiness](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/sba-form-1919-borrower-information/)). Form 413, the personal financial statement, is optional because lenders may use their own ([Fora Financial](https://www.forafinancial.com/blog/working-capital/sba-loan-forms/)). Sources conflict on who signs 1919 after a 2023 restructure, and on whether Form 1920 survives under SOP 50 10 8, which took effect June 1, 2025 ([Coleman SOP copy](https://colemanreport.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/SOP-50-10-8-effective-6.1.2025-Final.pdf)). Kubera should verify both on sba.gov before building.

For HUD 232/223(f), the broker assembles borrower and operator exhibits for the lender's underwriter. Each principal must supply previous-participation data on HUD-2530 ([HUD handbook](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/45611c5hsgh.pdf)), and ORCF has refused the paper form since 2017 in favor of the electronic APPS system ([HUD 2017 email blast](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/documents/Email-Blast-2008-May2017.pdf)). Refinances require audited financials ([Pease Bell](https://www.peasebell.com/insights/hud-232-refinancing/)), and 232 loans typically take **6 to 8 months** from application to close ([multifamily.loans](https://www.multifamily.loans/hud-232-lean-loans/)). That timeline is why HUD stage templates must differ from bridge templates. HUD also requires a software-based incident-reporting and risk-management system at each insured facility, an operator readiness item worth flagging at intake ([hud232.loan](https://www.hud232.loan/hud-232-lean-processing/)). Third-party reports matter across programs. SBA, HUD, Fannie and Freddie lenders typically require a Phase I ESA, traditionally contracted by the lender ([Curren Environmental](https://www.currenenvironmental.com/phase-i-advantages)), and lender-accepted Phase Is follow ASTM E1527-21 ([AEI Consultants](https://aeiconsultants.com/services/environmental-consulting/phase-i-esa/)). Kubera's planned vendor-order tracking should cover appraisal, Phase I, PCA/CNA, market study, survey, zoning, title, flood, and hotel PIP and franchise reports.

All of this converges on two modules Kubera has not planned. The first is a **sponsor and guarantor module**: REO schedule, a 413-style PFS, HUD-2530/APPS principal data, org chart and credit-authorization consent. The second is a **spreading layer** on top of the planned AI document agent. Extraction is a mature, cheap category: Clik.ai and Blooma read T-12s, rent rolls and operating statements ([Docsumo](https://www.docsumo.com/blog/best-cre-automation-tools)), and Minervian costs **$25 per seat per month** after five free deals ([Minervian](https://minervianai.com/best-cre-underwriting-tools)). The gap is the ratio layer. That layer normalizes NOI, computes DSCR at each lender's loan constant, debt yield and LTV per quote, and runs global cash flow across guarantors for SBA. SBA lenders split their tools between a system of record and a separate spreading and credit-memo layer ([CCG Catalyst](https://www.ccgcatalyst.com/thought-leadership/research-snapshot/sector-spotlight-sba-7a-underwriting-support-technology-for-loan-origination/)). A broker who delivers the second layer pre-built shortens the lender's work. Hotel deals need STR data in the same layer, and senior housing deals need census and payor mix.

## Buy commodity services, build the workflow

The economics favor a narrow integrate list. Sanctions and PEP screening costs cents per check: ComplyCube charges **$0.35–$0.50** ([ComplyCube](https://complycube.com/pricing)) and The KYB **$0.10 per AML verification** ([The KYB](https://thekyb.com/pricing)). Treasury publishes the OFAC SDN list free. Kubera should never build screening logic. It should call an API and write the result to the planned consent and audit ledger. E-sign is already planned through DocuSign. For reference, LendingPad passes DocuSign through at **$2.50 per envelope** ([LendingPad KB: DocuSign](https://kb.lendingpad.com/docusign-esign-instructions)), and Arive's e-sign supports no-login external signers ([Arive v7.2.0](https://support.arive.com/support/solutions/articles/61000306850-arive-release-notes-v7-2-0)). Kubera needs that external-signer support for guarantors and sponsors. Lender universe data can be rented. Lev's pricing page shows **$80, $200 and $400 per month** tiers with 1, 4 and 12 AI lender searches ([Lev pricing](https://www.lev.com/pricing)), though CRE Daily says subscriptions start around **$12k a year** ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/reviews/lev-review/)), and CommLoan's generator costs nothing. A free CommLoan second opinion costs Kubera nothing, so try it before paying Lev. Whether Lev or Janover expose APIs is unknown.

Everything else is cheaper to build than to find, because no vendor sells it for Kubera's programs. That includes program checklists, the outreach log, the fee ledger and Form 159, the spreading ratios, the sponsor module and the audit trail. Lender-grade platforms (nCino, Abrigo, Built, Lenders Cooperative) are quote-only and priced for banks ([aloan.ai](https://aloan.ai/guides/best-sba-lending-software)). Kubera should align its package output with what they ingest, such as 1919 field data, rather than buy them.

Three items need a decision gate before any build. California DRE obligations for commercial broker fee agreements, disclosures and trust funds were not researched and need counsel. Guarantor credit pulls raise FCRA permissible-purpose and authorization questions, and commercial lenders generally pull sponsor credit themselves, so Kubera should capture authorization consent and defer the pull. The current editions of Form 159 and Form 1920 need verification on sba.gov.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes where Kubera's differentiation lies. Residential broker platforms win on lender connectivity, which Kubera cannot replicate and does not need. Commercial broker platforms win on lender data breadth, which Kubera can rent. Neither covers the work that makes a HUD 232, SBA or hotel PIP deal close: program exhibits, principal data, global cash flow, STR and census spreads, Form 159 and fee accounting. That niche workflow is unserved by any vendor found. Combined with the AI advisor and document agents Kubera already has or plans, it is the defensible product. It is not a gap to backfill.

The sequencing implication is that the ledger and the outreach log come before more AI. Both are plain relational objects. Nearly every higher-order feature depends on them: Form 159, referral payouts, lender hit rates, fee forecasts, the audit trail and milestone notifications. Kubera's plan is AI-forward, and its agents need these records to act on. A document-chasing agent without program checklists, or a quote comparison without an outreach log behind it, will automate guesswork. Build the records first, then let the agents run on them.
