# Wholesale lender directory

Corin Commercial now has a working list of **434 lender desks** (384 distinct lenders) it can approach for broker accounts, in [`Wholesale lender directory.xlsx`](./Wholesale%20lender%20directory.xlsx). 63 are Tier A: the lender documents a broker channel (a broker page, portal or fee-protection statement, or for debt funds and banks, recent loans an outside intermediary arranged), lends in California, and the product fits. Each row carries loan size, leverage, rate, term, recourse, speed, the broker program, the sign-up link, phone, email, a publicly listed contact, licences, a fit note, a confidence grade and its sources. Yellow columns on the right are an outreach tracker, and the Dashboard counts progress by category and status. 16 rows are lenders already in Corin's routing table (`lib/ai/lenders.ts`) and start as "Existing relationship".

**Read this first:** the research environment could not open lender websites (the network policy blocked them), so every value comes from search-engine extracts of the lenders' own pages and trade press, not from pages opened directly. Confidence is graded on that basis: 23 High, 228 Medium, 183 Low. A second, independent pass re-checked every Tier A lender (72 checks: 27 verified, 28 corrected, 17 flagged for something to confirm on the first call). Its corrections are applied and its finding is in the "Second check" column; flagged lenders that lost their contact route or California evidence were moved down to Tier B. Confirm phone numbers, contacts and current terms on first contact.

## How the research was done

**Round 1 (2026-11-02)** covered six segments: HUD 232 and agency seniors housing; senior-living bridge, bank, construction and SBA; commercial construction debt funds and banks; wholesale investor lenders for ground-up and fix-and-flip; California private money; and national commercial bridge and hard money. A second pass worked through every name the first pass left unverified. **Round 2 (2026-11-03)** looked only for lenders not already listed, in eight more passes: multifamily; life insurance companies and CMBS conduits; hedge funds, private credit, mortgage REITs and family offices; hospitality and SBA hotel lenders; California community banks, credit unions, SBA 504 CDCs and 7(a) lenders; more construction and hard money; more senior living; and a follow-up on names the first wave could not verify. The "Added" column shows each row's round. Each record follows one schema (`research_notes/Wholesale lender directory/SCHEMA.md`), with a no-guessing rule: a blank cell means the value was not published, and phone numbers, emails, URLs and licence numbers appear only where a source showed them verbatim. Duplicate rows for the same lender were merged, conflicting findings were resolved in `overrides.json` with the reason recorded, and the workbook is rebuilt by `build_workbook.py` from those files.

## Caveats

- **Search extracts, not page reads.** Lender sites were blocked from the research environment, so figures are as quoted in search results. Several lenders publish conflicting numbers across pages; the fit note says so where it was seen.
- **Terms move.** Rates, leverage and minimums are a screen, not a quote. Get the current matrix from the lender.
- **Banks and agency lenders rarely publish a broker program,** so most sit in Tier B or C. Debt funds that publish none but close loans an outside intermediary arranged can still be Tier A, and their broker-channel cell cites that deal. On HUD 232 loans an outside broker is typically paid out of the lender's own financing fee rather than as a separate charge; confirm the split with each lender.
- **Contacts change.** Named contacts are people each lender lists publicly in an originations or broker role.
- **Compliance.** The Account Setup sheet repeats this repo's counsel research on the MLDS (RE 882), table funding and the broker usury exemption. That research is not legal advice and a California attorney must confirm it.
