# Expand three loan lines without touching funds

Corin Commercial can add hard money, deeper multifamily and construction lending mostly by extending software it already has: the sizer, the Jev rules engine, the document vault, the checklists and the agent stack. The parts it should not build are the regulated money-handling layers: trust accounting, loan servicing, investor fund administration and construction-draw disbursement. It should integrate or outsource those, and it should not take custody of investor or construction funds until California counsel clears each path. Two findings need action now. First, **the sizer prices every HUD multifamily quote on pre-2025 rules**: 85% leverage at 1.176x and 0.60–0.65% annual MIP, where current HUD rules allow 87% at 1.15x (90% at 1.11x for affordable deals) with a flat 0.25% MIP. Second, **the Mortgage Loan Disclosure Statement (MLDS) applies to commercial loans**. The DRE's own advisory says it covers any type of real property, so Corin's private bridge line probably needs an RE 882 on every loan it arranges today. No single vendor serves a commercial broker across these three lines. Private-lending suites (The Mortgage Office, Baseline, LendingWise, Liquid Logics, Mortgage Automator) and draw platforms (Built, Land Gorilla, Rabbet) are built for the lender of record and would duplicate Corin's pipeline. The work that differentiates Corin is therefore broker-side: hard-money and construction sizing, California rent-control and lien-waiver rules, rent-roll and budget extraction, and a draw-package pre-check that never touches the money. For e-signature, **BoldSign** costs about $37.50 a month at 50 envelopes and $150 at 200, against DocuSign API tiers that start at $300 once volume passes about 40 envelopes. The consolidated gap table at the end assigns each item to a build phase.

A caveat on sources: the research team could not open leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, dre.ca.gov or most vendor sites from its environment. Every statute reading, threshold and vendor price below comes from search-result snippets of the cited pages, or from third-party price directories that often disagree. California statute numbers and dollar thresholds must be checked against the current code before Corin relies on them. The code findings come from reading `lib/sizing/assumptions.ts`, `lib/ai/knowledge.ts` and `components/home/Bento.tsx` in the repository.

## The sizer quotes HUD multifamily on rules HUD retired in January 2025

HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 changed market-rate 223(f) and 221(d)(4) from **1.176x DSCR and 85% LTV/LTC to 1.15x and 87%** ([Dwight Capital](https://dwightcapital.com/news/hud-announces-beneficial-changes-in-dscr-and-ltc-ltv-requirements-for-fha/); [NewPoint](https://newpoint.com/news/hud-announces-changes-dscr-and-ltcltv-requirements)). Affordable and rental-assistance properties now get **90% at 1.11x** ([hud.loans](https://www.hud.loans/fha-223f/); [CohnReznick](https://www.cohnreznick.com/insights/multifamily-housing-programs)). The Federal Register final notice of September 23, 2025 then set the MIP for all FHA multifamily insurance programs at the **0.25% statutory minimum** and removed the separate green and affordable tiers. The notice applies to applications submitted or amended on or after October 1, 2025 ([Federal Register 2025-18379](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/23/2025-18379/changes-in-mortgage-insurance-premiums-applicable-to-fha-multifamily-insurance-programs); [Novogradac](https://www.novoco.com/news/hud-reduces-mips-to-025-for-fha-multifamily-insurance-programs)). Corin's code still carries the old values.

## Hard money is a sizing and compliance build, not a software purchase

Private and hard money loans are short-term, interest-only balloon loans sized on collateral, not on the borrower's global cash flow. One lender-data summary puts the Q1 2026 average rate at **9.95% with 2.96 points** ([Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/hard-money-loan-rates-2026)). Fix-and-flip leverage is commonly **65–75% of after-repair value (ARV)** ([Wonder Rates](https://wonderrates.com/learning-hub/hard-money-loans-vs-bridge-loans)). Terms run 6–24 months for hard money and 12–36 months for bridge ([Slate Financial](https://www.slatefinancial.io/blog/wp-bridge-loan-vs-hard-money-loan-2026)). The economics sit in the back-end terms. Extensions cost 0.5–1% for each 3–6 months. Default interest is often the greater of the note rate and prime plus 3%, and some agreements in public lenders' filings reach 24%. Some lenders require 3–6 months of minimum interest and charge exit fees of 0.5–1% ([Crowdfunded Wealth](https://www.crowdfundedwealth.com/articles/hard-money-loan-extension-default-interest-points-2026); [OfferMarket](https://www.offermarket.us/blog/hard-money-loan-term-sheet)). Underwriting rests on ARV, a scope of work that reconciles to that ARV, a borrower track record that sets the leverage tier, and a time-bound exit backed by takeout evidence ([Biz2Credit](https://biz2credit.com/hard-money-loans/requirements-guide); [Capital Funding](https://capitalfunding.com/blog/hard-money-lender-approval-criteria-top-10-factors/)). Most published figures are marketing from residential-investor lenders. The research found no sourced 2026 benchmarks for commercial land LTV, second-lien CLTV or interest-reserve sizing.

Corin's sizer cannot express any of this. Its `BRIDGE.cre` box is a single 75% LTV, 7% debt-yield, 24-month product (`lib/sizing/assumptions.ts` L115). It has no ARV, no loan-to-cost with a rehab holdback, no interest reserve, no extension options, no default rate and no minimum interest. Those parameters, and the underwriting records behind them (valuation, line-item budget, track-record schedule, exit analysis), are Corin's to build. The track-record schedule belongs inside the sponsor and guarantor module the earlier research already specified. California adds a constraint the sizer must enforce. When a broker places investor money, B&P §10232.3 caps LTV at **80% of market value for improved property, 50% for unimproved land and 65% for a single-family-zoned lot**, parcel by parcel ([DRE RE 7 manual](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true)). The research could not confirm whether these caps reach every broker-arranged investor loan.

### Three capital models carry very different regulatory loads

A California broker can fund private loans in three ways. Under **table funding**, a capital partner funds the loan at closing, and a DRE-licensed originator must disclose who actually funds and who will hold the deed of trust ([Private Lender Link](https://privatelenderlink.com/guide/table-funding/)). Under **whole or fractional trust deeds**, the broker sells notes to individual investors. The DRE multi-lender rules (§§10237–10238) exempt these from securities qualification only if a note has no more than ten investors, each of whom meets net-worth or income tests ([DRE Reference Book ch. 10](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/refbook/ref10.pdf)). Under a **pooled fund**, an LLC or LP issues securities and lends from its own balance sheet. Corin already advertises white-label table funding, so the lowest-friction sequence is to stay there first. Whole and fractional placements can follow once investor onboarding, trust-fund and servicing workflows exist. A Corin-sponsored fund should be treated as a separate securities project. Investor onboarding is a regulated workflow, not a CRM contact type. Before the broker accepts any funds, the investor must sign an RE 851A (new loan) or RE 851B (existing note), plus an RE 851D for multi-property loans ([DRE MLB forms](https://dre.ca.gov/Forms/MLB.html); [Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/private-trust-deed-lending-disclosure-and-dre-filing-summary/)). Suitability runs through the RE 870 questionnaire, and qualification statements must be kept for four years ([DRE RE 7](https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re7.pdf?TB_iframe=true&TB_iframe=true)). The investor also attests that the investment does not exceed 10% of net worth ([Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/doss-guides/alternatives-to-the-multi-lender-rule/)). Finally, the loan file must declare which code section or securities exemption the deal relies on ([Justia §10236.7](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-5/section-10236-7/)).

## Seven California questions must reach counsel before any code ships

The single most important correction in this research is about the MLDS. The DRE's MLDS advisory says a broker must give the borrower an RE 882 (or the RE 885 nontraditional form) under B&P §10240 for a loan on **any type of real property, including commercial property and raw land**. The form is due within **three business days of the written application, or before the borrower becomes obligated, whichever is earlier** ([DRE MLDS advisory](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/adv/2015_2019/Advisory2015MLDS%20and%20Loan%20Estimate%20Advisory.pdf)). §10245 exempts most of Article 7 for first liens of $30,000 or more, but it expressly carves §10240 back in ([FindLaw §10245](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-10245/)). The signed acknowledgment must be kept for three years ([Justia §10240](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-4/part-1/chapter-3/article-5/section-10240/)). The earlier research assumed the MLDS was a consumer-loan form. On this evidence it is not. Corin should assume an MLDS is required on every loan it negotiates as broker, including today's bridge and table-funded deals, unless counsel identifies a specific exemption.

The usury claim needs the same care. Corin's homepage (`components/home/Bento.tsx` L76) and advisor primer (`lib/ai/knowledge.ts` L35) state flatly that Corin-arranged loans are "exempt from state usury limits under Civil Code § 1916.1." Courts apply that exemption only where the broker actually arranged the loan and was compensated or expected compensation, not where the broker's name was lent to the deal ([White & Bright](https://www.whiteandbright.com/california-usury-law-exemptions-for-real-estate-brokers-2/); [Doss Law](https://www.dosslaw.com/definitive-guides/doss-law-llps-definitive-guide-to-usury-in-california/)). *In re Moon* held that modifying a broker-arranged loan held by an unlicensed lender could make it usurious ([CLA](https://calawyers.org/business-law/in-re-moon-bankr-n-d-cal/)). SB 1146, effective January 1, 2025, extends §1916.1 to forbearances, extensions and modifications that a licensed broker arranges ([GoDocs](https://godocs.com/in-re-moon-update-forthcoming-changes-to-california-civil-code-1916-1/)). The California Mortgage Association warns that a modification negotiated by an unlicensed servicer may still be exposed ([CMA, June 2025](https://www.californiamortgageassociation.org/2025/06/05/ongoing-risks-when-modifying-loans-post-sb-1146/)). The platform should therefore record Corin as arranging broker, with its compensation, on every loan and every extension, and the site copy should state the exemption's conditions.

## Multifamily depth comes from data and California rules, not new lenders

Multifamily capital is abundant in 2026. FHFA raised each GSE's purchase cap to **$88B**, at least half of it mission-driven, and workforce-housing loans sit outside the cap ([FHFA](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/u.s.-federal-housing-announces-2026-multifamily-loan-purchase-caps-for-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac)). Fannie Mae's small-loan term sheet offers up to **$9M at 80% LTV and 1.25x** with 5–30-year terms ([Fannie Mae](https://multifamily.fanniemae.com/media/37046/display)), which matches Corin's single agency box. Real deals rarely reach that maximum: CBRE's Q2 2026 averages, reported secondhand, were **63.3% LTV, 1.43x DSCR and a 10.2% debt yield** ([YieldStack](https://yieldstack.ai/blog/multifamily-bridge-loan-ltv)). Most deals are DSCR-constrained, so the sizer should run tiered grids with selectable terms rather than one box. Bridge lenders go to 65–75% of as-is value, and some reach 85–90% loan-to-cost with a renovation holdback. They gate the loan on an exit test, typically a **9.5–10.5% stabilized debt yield and 1.25x at a takeout rate** ([YieldStack](https://yieldstack.ai/blog/multifamily-bridge-loan-ltv); [bridgeloan-financing](https://bridgeloan-financing.com/multifamily-bridge-loans-guide/)). Corin's bridge box has no exit test. DSCR investor loans on five or more units run 70–75% LTV and 1.00–1.25x with FICO floors around 680–700 ([dscrcapitalpartners](https://dscrcapitalpartners.com/blog/dscr-ltv-limits/)). That box is a natural on-ramp from multifamily to the hard money line. On referrals to DUS and Optigo lenders, brokers typically negotiate **0.50–0.75% paid by the lender**, or 0.75–1.50% borrower-paid, and some DUS lenders refuse to work with brokers ([Janover](https://janover.pro/guides/broker-fee-structures)). The lender panel needs a broker-friendliness flag, and the fee ledger needs both payer structures.

The lender package is the same across programs: rent roll, T-12 (often an annualized T-3 against T-12 expenses), comps, expense benchmarks, a re-assessed tax line, insurance, reserves and the sponsor schedule. Only comps, benchmarks, tax rates, parcel data and rent-control status can be pulled automatically. The rest comes from the borrower and must be extracted and normalized. The earlier research already specified a spreading and ratio layer. This research adds the multifamily-specific haircuts it must apply and the data that feeds them.

California makes rent growth a rules problem. AB 1482 caps annual increases at 5% plus regional CPI, with a 10% maximum. The cap is **8.7% in Los Angeles for August 2026 through July 2027** ([Nourmand](https://nourmand.com/blog/ab-1482-rent-increases-in-2026-what-la-landlords-can-actually-charge)). Local ordinances bind harder: the City of Los Angeles RSO allows **3%** for July 2026 through June 2027 ([financialsupportresources](https://www.financialsupportresources.com/post/los-angeles-rent-increase-rules-rso-3-percent-ab-1482-cap-notice-2026-27)), and unincorporated LA County allows 1.919% ([lametrohomefinder](https://www.lametrohomefinder.com/blog/los-angeles-rent-increase-rules)). Costa-Hawkins permits vacancy decontrol ([costa-hawkins.com](https://costa-hawkins.com/ab-1482/)). In a rent-controlled building, a bridge business plan's mark-to-market upside therefore comes only from unit turnover. A per-unit rent-control classifier is a deterministic engine Corin can build from parcel year-built data and a yearly-updated rate table. No vendor in the research sells one, and it would make Corin's California business plans more credible to lenders than a generic national model. Prop 13 re-assessment on acquisition belongs beside it. The mechanics are standard, but the research did not source them, so the tax-rate-area logic needs its own verification. SB 9 and SB 330 and the San Francisco and other city ordinances were not researched.

## Construction value sits before closing and around the draw, never inside it

Construction leverage spans a wide range. Banks lend **60–65% of cost at SOFR plus 275–350 basis points**, and debt funds reach 65–75% at SOFR plus 425–525 ([COR Advisors](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/05/construction-loan-rates-2026.html)). HUD 221(d)(4) now allows 87% market-rate and 90% affordable. Private ground-up lenders go to **85–90% LTC, tiered by completed projects and capped at 70–75% of ARV**, and size on the lesser of the two ([New Silver](https://newsilver.com/ground-up/construction-loans-california/); [Park Place](https://parkplacefinance.com/?p=6164)). SBA 504 splits 50/40/10 and needs an interim lender. That lender must sign SBA Form 2288 no more than 60 days before the debenture funds ([SBA Form 2288](https://www.sba.gov/document/sba-form-2288-interim-lender-certification)). Fannie's unfunded forward commitment allows 30 months for construction plus lease-up ([Fannie Mae MF Guide](https://mfguide.fanniemae.com/fnmf-pdf/download/15546)), and Freddie's advances no construction funds ([Freddie Ch. 17A](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/chapters/mf_guide_ch_17A.pdf)). C-PACE caps are set by each program, and no California figure was found. The gap between a 60–65% bank senior loan and 85–90% total capitalization is where a broker earns the most for structuring. Corin's sizer cannot show it. `BRIDGE.construction` is one 80% LTC box (`lib/sizing/assumptions.ts` L116), and a search of the codebase found no interest reserve, contingency, retainage or ARV cap anywhere. The construction sizer needs an interest reserve funded inside LTC (project cost equals land plus budget plus funded interest ([PPF Term Sheet Builder](https://ppf-pricing.pages.dev/))), contingency and developer-fee inputs, lesser-of sizing with experience tiers, a capital-stack view and a take-out test against the permanent loan.

After closing, draw administration belongs to the lender, its servicer or a fund-control agent. HUD's process shows how much that involves. Retainage is 10% of each advance until 50% completion, may then step down to 5% and later to 2.5%, and is released only after cost certification and a final 100% inspection ([MAP Guide §12.15.5](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/federal/us-hud-map-guide-4430-g-2020-rev2021-03-19/12.15.5-amount-of-contractor-s-retainage-and-release); [§12.7.7](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/federal/us-hud-map-guide-4430-g-2020-rev2021-03-19/12.7.7-final-advance)). The broker's legitimate role is the package around the draw: assembling AIA G702/G703 pay applications, invoices, waivers, stored-materials proof and change orders, pre-checking them against the lender's budget, scheduling the inspector, and flagging contingency and interest-reserve burn before the project goes out of balance. An out-of-balance project is also a brokerage trigger, because it creates demand for rescue mezzanine debt, loan increases and extensions. Built's own **AI Draw Agent starts in a read-only "audit mode"** ([Built](https://getbuilt.com/ai)). Corin can offer the same read-only review from the borrower's side with its Claude extraction and Inngest workflows. Built reports cutting residential draw timelines from about seven to about three business days ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/built)); that is a vendor-sourced figure.

California law shapes the coordination product. The four statutory lien waivers, Civ. Code §§8132, 8134, 8136 and 8138, must "follow, substantially" the statutory forms ([CSLB](https://cslb.ca.gov/consumers/legal_issues_for_consumers/Mechanics_Lien/Conditional_And_Unconditional_Waiver_Release_Form.aspx)). A waiver module must generate all four, enforce conditional-before-payment and unconditional-after-funds-clear for each payee on each draw, and route them through e-sign. A construction lender that receives a bonded stop payment notice must withhold enough to cover the claim ([Civ. Code §8536](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_8536)), so lien-alert intake should flow from the title company or a monitoring service. Prevailing wage can attach to private projects that receive forgiven or contingently repaid public loans ([AB 2272 analysis](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/asm/ab_2251-2300/ab_2272_bill_20140903_enrolled.htm); [Allen Matkins](https://allenmatkins.com/real-ideas/public-subsidies-not-allowing-the-tail-to-wag-the-dog.html)). A Jev rule should flag any public subsidy before the term sheet goes out. The Escrow Law (gate E) is the hard line. Corin must never receive or disburse construction funds. Whether paid coordination without custody falls outside the "joint control agent" definition is the single legal opinion that the draw product depends on.

The construction notes and the backend notes disagreed on draws: one proposed building a borrower-side draw-package builder, the other proposed skipping any draw engine. Both positions hold once the work is split by role. Corin should **skip the lender-side engine** (funding, retainage release, title date-downs, disbursement) and **build the borrower-side pre-check**, which handles documents and never money. The pre-check ships only after counsel clears gate E.

## Agents need gates, evals and an event store before autonomy

Lev is now Corin's closest rival on the broker workflow. September 2026 coverage calls it "an operating system for CRE finance" spanning intake, extraction, underwriting, lender research, outreach and closing ([PlotDaily](https://plotdaily.com/stories/lev-review-run-cre-financing-from-one-ai-platform)). Its "Lev Agent" answers questions from the user's own deals and drafts materials ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lev/id6747097952)). In October 2026 it launched "Source," an AI deal-sourcing product ([AI for CRE Collective](https://aiforcrecollective.com/stories/lev-launches-ai-deal-sourcing-platform)). Directory-listed pricing is $80, $200 and $400 a month ([AI for CRE Collective](https://aiforcrecollective.com/tools/lev)). Generic CRE debt search is therefore commoditized. On the lender side, the agents that work are narrow and tied to SOPs: Casca for SBA intake and eligibility, Aloan for source-cited credit memos, Built for draws ([Aloan](https://aloan.ai/products/commercial); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/06/built-technologies-ai-draw-agent-cre-lending.html)). The research found **no vendor offering condition-clearing or closing-checklist agents for brokers**, and none that sends lender submissions without human review. Corin's broker approval gate matches market practice. Its open space lies in niche program depth and in the post-term-sheet work no one has automated.

The backend pattern is settled. Inngest's `step.waitForEvent()` pauses a run until a matching approval event arrives, with a timeout, and Inngest's documentation says to handle approved, rejected and no-response outcomes ([Inngest HITL](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/durable-agents/human-in-the-loop)). It can gate only the risky tools inside an agent loop ([Inngest durable agents](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/durable-agents)). Current guides list the requirements for durable agents as an execution journal, **idempotent tool boundaries, versioned prompts and tools, durable human approvals and recovery tests** ([zylos.ai](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-24-durable-execution-agent-runtimes)). Temporal offers the same primitives ([Temporal](https://temporal.io/blog/temporal-agent-harness-durable-agent-infrastructure)), but a second engine adds nothing at Corin's scale. Built's graduated "audit, assist, automate" modes are the template for letting low-risk actions, such as document reminders, leave the approval gate over time. The new lines raise the stakes. An agent that drafts an MLDS, an RE 851A or a lien waiver produces a regulated record. A timed-out approval must therefore expire rather than send, and high-risk actions should require the approver to re-authenticate.

This research changes the earlier audit-trail item. Inngest and Temporal histories are vendor-hosted with limited retention. Corin needs its own insert-only, optionally hash-chained `agent_events` store that records the model, the prompt and tool versions, the inputs (by vault document ID), the approver and the external message ID. Retention should be at least three years, matching the MLDS rule, and four years for investor qualification records. The KYC item changes too: integrated OFAC screening needs an evidence record (list version, match score, disposition, reviewer) and rescreening before closing, extended to investors once Phase 4 begins. For data, the cheap building blocks suffice. OpenCorporates starts at about £2,250 a year for entity checks ([OpenCorporates](https://opencorporates.com/pricing/)), and no official California bizfile API exists ([mcp.so](https://mcp.so/servers/bizfile-mcp)). Ocrolus, with a reported median contract near $98,000 a year, is oversized for a broker ([lenderanalyzer](https://lenderanalyzer.com/plaid-pricing)). Because the ADMT duties may begin on January 1, 2027, two months from now, the AI-use notice, the human-review opt-out and an access-request export are cheap insurance to ship in Phase 1, while counsel decides whether the CCPA covers Corin at all.

The legal picture supports the switch. California UETA (Civ. Code §1633.1 et seq.) and ESIGN cover engagement and fee agreements, NDAs, referral splits and authorizations. California notaries cannot perform remote online notarization until the Secretary of State finishes its technology project, with **January 1, 2030 as the outside date** ([CA SOS Notary Handbook](https://notary.cdn.sos.ca.gov/forms/notary-handbook-current.pdf); [californianotaryprep](https://www.californianotaryprep.com/remote-online-notarization-california-2026/)). Out-of-state remote notarization at about $25 per session is recognized under Civ. ## Conclusion

The three new lines are lopsided in where the risk sits. Multifamily is a data-and-rules problem: Corin can deepen it safely with no counsel gate beyond its existing ones, and its biggest single gain is fixing constants that are already wrong. Construction and hard money are the reverse. The software is easy, but the legal perimeter decides which software is worth writing at all. One perimeter question governs each line. For hard money, Corin must decide whether it will ever hold investor money. For construction draws, it must decide whether it will ever touch disbursements. Answering "not yet" to both lets Corin ship the hard money sizer, the MLDS, the broker-of-record trail, lien waivers and the draw pre-check in the first three phases. Trust accounting, investor portals and servicing stay in Phase 4 behind integrations that can be bought later.

The MLDS finding also shows that "business-purpose, so exempt" was an assumption, not a finding, and it may be wrong elsewhere. The earlier research dropped the residential compliance stack because Corin's loans are business-purpose. That still holds for TRID and HMDA. California's broker statutes, however, regulate the broker's conduct regardless of the loan's purpose. Before Corin builds deal-desk records for these lines, its compliance model should be re-based on what the broker does (arranges, places notes, services, coordinates draws) rather than on what the loan is. That framing also gives the agents their limits. An agent can draft the MLDS, flag the threshold count and pre-check the draw. A licensed human must sign off, and the money must never pass through Corin.
