# How Kubera works with borrowers

Source: Kubera Commercial's own description of its service. It restates what the program primer already says so retrieval can ground answers about roles, disclosures and timing. It is not legal advice.

## Kubera's role: broker, not lender
Kubera Commercial, Inc. is a California DRE-licensed real estate corporation that arranges commercial real estate loans as a broker. It is not the lender. The lender, bank, agency, HUD-approved lender or private lender funds the loan at closing as the lender of record. Kubera does not fund loans, accept or hold borrower or investor money, service loans, or take any role in construction draws. Kubera is compensated as disclosed to the borrower, and the amounts appear on the lender's closing statement.

## What an advisor does
Kubera's service is advisor-led and AI-assisted. The AI helps structure questions, size indicative scenarios and draft indicative term sheets; a licensed advisor reviews every engagement and every message that goes to a borrower or a lender. Indicative figures are estimates for discussion. They are not a commitment to lend, an approval, or a rate lock.

## Mortgage Loan Disclosure Statement
For loans Kubera arranges that are secured by real property, California requires the broker to give the borrower a Mortgage Loan Disclosure Statement (DRE form RE 882). It describes the estimated borrower-paid costs, all broker compensation including any paid by the lender, and the loan terms. The borrower signs it before becoming obligated on the loan. This applies to commercial loans as well as residential ones. Your advisor will confirm what applies to your loan.

## Timing
How long a loan takes depends on the program, the lender, the property and how complete the file is. Many private bridge and hard money loans close in 10 to 21 days after a complete file, subject to lender approval, appraisal, title and diligence. HUD financing is much longer: HUD LEAN processing is typically 120 to 180 days. Timing is never guaranteed.

## What a complete file usually includes
Trailing twelve-month operating statements, a current rent roll or census, the borrower's personal financial statement and real estate schedule, recent tax returns, an entity chart, the purchase contract or existing loan documents, and property reports ordered by the lender such as appraisal, environmental, property condition and survey. The exact list depends on the program and is tracked as a checklist in the borrower portal.
